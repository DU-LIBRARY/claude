The Fiqh of Forex

“Ṣarf, in the usage of the Sharīʿah, is the name for selling absolute prices for one another: gold for gold, silver for silver, and either kind for the other.”

al-Kāsānī, Badāʾiʿ al-Ṣanāʾiʿ, as cited in Fiqh al-Buyūʿ by Mufti Muhammad Taqi Usmani, §312–337

Fiqh al-Buyūʿ treats ṣarf as the third type of sale when sales are divided by the nature of the two counter-values. Its distinguishing rules are why it is given its own chapter: equality is required when both sides are of one kind; deferral is not allowed whether the kinds are the same or different; mutual possession must happen in the session (which, for the Ḥanafīs, separates ṣarf from other ribawi goods like wheat and barley); and a stipulated option is barred by agreement, because it prevents real possession. This deck follows the chapter: the conditions, then paper money, then the author’s ruling on Forex.

§312 · Definition

Price sold for price

Gold and silver are prices by their creation (athmān khilqiyya). Selling one for the other, or for itself, is ṣarf.

§314 · Condition 1

التقابض في المجلس

Hand to hand, in the session

Both sides are possessed before the parties part bodily. Making it available (takhliya) is not enough, and not even an hour’s delay is allowed.

“Gold for gold is riba, except hand to hand.”al-Bukhārī 2174, from ʿUmar
Valid ṣarf
Void: they parted before possession
Evidence: “Gold for gold is riba except hand to hand (hāʾa wa hāʾa)” (al-Bukhārī 2174, from ʿUmar), and “Do not sell gold for gold except like for like… and do not sell what is absent for what is present” (al-Bukhārī 2177, from Abū Saʿīd al-Khudrī). Parting means parting with the bodies: if they stay in their session, however long, there is no parting; if one calls the other from behind a wall, the ṣarf is not valid. Takhliya is enough for possession in other sales for the Ḥanafīs, but not in ṣarf, by consensus. Al-Durr al-Mukhtār: “taqābuḍ with the fingers, not by takhliya.” Ibn ʿAbd al-Barr: “No delay in any ṣarf is allowed, not even an hour.” (Press → to show a party leaving before possession.)

§316 · Condition 2

التماثل في صرف المتجانسين

Like for like, within one kind

Gold for gold, silver for silver: equal weight, even if quality and workmanship differ.

Exchanging by estimate (mujāzafa) is barred by consensus.

Equal weight: valid
Riba: excess within one kind
Tamāthul (§316) applies only when both sides are of one kind. Quality and workmanship do not count: wrought gold against coined gold is still weighed like for like. (Press → to tip the scale to 10 g against 12 g.)

§317 · Condition 3

الخلو عن خيار الشرط

No option to cancel

A stipulated option delays full ownership, and so breaks complete possession.

The option for defects still applies. The option of inspection applies to wrought pieces, not to coined dirhams and dinars.

Barred in ṣarf
A stipulated option (khiyār al-sharṭ) is barred by agreement because it prevents real possession: ownership is not complete while one party may still cancel. The option for defects (khiyār al-ʿayb) remains. The option of inspection applies to wrought pieces, not to coined dirhams and dinars. (Press → to strike the option clause.)

§328 · Paper money

What is a banknote?

Position 1

A receipt for debt

Notes are bonds of debt, so exchanging them would be impossible without real taqābuḍ.

Position 2

Gold and silver

Notes take the full ruling of gold and silver, with every condition of ṣarf.

The author’s viewPosition 3

A conventional price

أثمان اصطلاحية

Made a price by custom and law, closest to circulating fulūs. Each country’s currency is its own kind.

The book surveys how contemporaries treat paper money. Some called notes bonds of debt, which would make exchange impossible without real taqābuḍ. Others gave them the full ruling of gold and silver. The author’s preferred “third position”: notes have become conventional prices by custom and law, closest to the circulating fulūs (al-fulūs al-nāfiqa). (Press → to bring the author’s view forward.)

§328 · Applying the third position

Exchanging currencies

Exchanging the same currency (rupee for rupee) with deferral is not allowed by agreement of the Ḥanafī imams, because a single kind alone forbids nasīʾah. Exchanging different currencies (Pakistani rupee for Saudi riyal), by analogy with Imam Muḥammad’s view on fulūs, allows both excess and deferral, provided one of the two sides is taken in the session. Where one side is deferred, the exchange must be at the rate of the day of the contract, so it does not become a back door to riba (p. 713). On rates set by law: if a government fixes an exchange rate, it should be followed, though breaching it is not itself riba (p. 702).

The author’s words · p. 713

«فيجوز فيها التفاضلُ والنسيئةُ جميعاً، بشرط أن يُقبَض أحدُ البدلين في المجلس، لئلّا يؤدّي إلى الافتراق عن دَينٍ بدَين»

Excess and deferral are both allowed, provided one side is taken in the session, so the parties never part holding debt against debt.

§337 · Where it fails

Five problems

  1. 1

    Money turned into merchandise

    Currency exists to value things, not to be traded for itself; doing so has damaged the financial system.

  2. 2

    No possession in the session

    “Spot” deals settle two days later and run by computer, so not even one currency is taken in the session.

  3. 3

    Selling what one does not own

    Often nothing is delivered or received; buyers and sellers simply settle the price differences.

  4. 4

    Sale on margin

    Deposit a little, trade a lot; the exchange guarantees the rest for a commission. In reality, a riba loan.

  5. 5

    Currencies never specified

    Currency is specified (taʿyīn) only by possession, which is missing in most of these operations.

From §337 (pp. 727–728). One: currencies are a means of valuing things and not sought for themselves; the Sharīʿah does not favour making them the object of trade except for a real need to exchange, which is why ṣarf carries its conditions. The market made currency itself the object of trade, which has caused great corruption in the modern financial system (he refers to his paper on the causes of the financial crisis). Two: many contemporary scholars and fiqh academies give paper currency the ruling of gold and silver, requiring taqābuḍ in the session, which is missing, since the market divides into spot and forward sales and even the “spot sale” settles only two days after the contract. On the author’s preferred third position, one of the two currencies must be taken in the session, and that too is missing, since dealing is by computer or internet. Three: people often sell what they do not own, with no delivery; they settle price differences. Four: margin trading is in reality a loan on which interest is charged. Five: currencies bought are not specified, since specification of currencies happens only by possession. (Press → to step through each problem.)

The ruling · §337

فإنّه لا يجوز التعامل شرعاً عن طريق سوق الفوريكس

Dealing through the Forex market is not permissible in the Sharīʿah.

Genuine need for travel or trade is met by ordinary money changers, within the conditions of ṣarf.

Conclusion: it is not permissible to deal through the Forex market. Genuine need for travel or international trade is met through ordinary buying and selling with money changers, keeping to the conditions above.

Recap

Ṣarf in three lines

  • Hand to hand in every ṣarf, before the parties part.
  • Like for like when both sides are one kind.
  • No option to cancel may be stipulated.
