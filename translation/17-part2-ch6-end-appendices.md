## Appendix A: The Components of an Islamic Constitution

### Introduction

[p. 321] It has become [the case] today that most states of the world have their own special constitution. And what is most fitting, in view of what we mentioned earlier of the principles of Islamic politics and its rulings, is that they be codified, so that they are included in the constitution of the state, so that they become clear and evident to the common people and the elite. And no wonder; for many of them are fit to be included within the constitution. And a long period before — more than forty years — I had written two studies: in one of them I clarified the components of the Islamic constitution in the Qur'an, and in the other I clarified the requirements of the Islamic constitution in Pakistan in view of its circumstances and situations. And because of their connection with the subject of our book, we add them to it as two appendices, A and B.

### A look at the constitution

The constitution of the state in reality forms that first brick on which the structure of the system of government in it is built, and on which its existence is founded. And it is the pole around which the mill of the state's development and its flourishing, its consolidation and its prosperity revolves; and it is the root on which the people's coexistence in the state with safety, peace, calm and prosperity depends. So if this first basic brick of the state were laid crooked, then say farewell to its system and its development: "Peace!"

It is known that the constitution is applied to the collection of rulings that clarify the form of the state and its basic goals and the system of rule in it, its authorities and the manner of distributing these authorities, and the clarification of their competences; and the clarification of the rights of citizens and their duties; and other than that of main rulings, like the system of forming the government, and the manner of electing the president and the members of parliament, and their conditions and their qualities and the way of removing them; and the clarification of who takes the authority of legislation and its powers; and the competences of the courts, and the definition of the laws by which it rules in pleadings and disputes; and the manner of appointing employees in the governmental departments, and the distribution of their functions among the units

[p. 322] of the state and its territories; and the clarification of the bases on which the budget of the state and its allocations will be arranged; and the principles of imposing taxes on the people; and how the regional governments are formed, and to what extent their powers will be, and which are the matters in which the reference will be entirely to the central government; and to what extent it will be permitted to overturn the existence of the government in the state of emergency, and specifically the state of war with another state; and in the like of this state, what are the special privileges which the governmental bodies will be able to use.

So these matters are clarified and defined in the constitution of the state, and then its apparatuses are administered on it throughout the period of its remaining. And after the constitution is implemented and applied to the state once, it is obligatory on the government that it perform all its works and its duties in the light of that constitution and in full subordination to it. So if it did not do that and undertook what contradicts the constitution of measures, or issued what contradicts it of rulings, the people would be able to cancel it by way of appeal in the Supreme Court. But if there were included in the constitution itself erroneous clauses that clash with the interests of the subjects and conflict with them, then the government would be, however it may be, compelled to act by it, without there being in the hands of the people the option to reform it or a way to get rid of its evil consequences and its miserable ends — except amendment in the constitution, and it is of difficulty and impossibility in such a place. The Pakistani nation, which was struck because of the amendment in the 1962 constitution with heavy losses which cannot be compensated nor mended at all, estimates very well the extent of its difficulty and its hardship; and it still suffers crises and endures problems and faces difficulties because of that erroneous constitution, which was implemented once and continued its harming of the state and its interests repeatedly.

So laying down the constitution for the state which is resolved to begin its work from 3 March requires that those undertaking it have an ample share of far-sightedness, breadth of horizon, cleverness and intelligence — alongside deliberation and reflection, arduous effort and serious attempt. And the work of laying down the constitution, even if the direct contribution in it is for the members of parliament and the house of representatives, yet it is never in the interest of the people that it overlook it, ignoring [and] feigning blindness; rather it is touched by a need for complete alertness and full understanding

[p. 323] in all stages of its accomplishment.

And from this, the people only chose the members representing it so that they undertake, on its behalf, laying down a constitution that realizes its interests and fulfils all its requirements and meets its needs. So if they did not undertake that, the people's trust in them would be shaken, and they would have the right to question them, and then to carry them to perform their responsibilities in laying down a constitution according to what pleases it and delights it.

It is a self-evident, accepted phenomenon that does not need proof nor require evidence: that Pakistan only succeeds in implementing in it a constitution that agrees with the teachings of the Sharia. And this is what the head of state declared in a legal memorandum for the constitution; just as all the political parties acknowledged the pressing need for it during the campaign of propaganda for the past parliamentary elections, and promised the nation to contribute their share and give their buckets in founding it on purely, pure Islamic foundations and principles. And no wonder in this; for the state has raised its banner from Islam, so there is no room in it nor scope for laying down or implementing a non-Islamic constitution.

And what concerns us in this field is the role of the party that has won the majority of votes in parliament, and then has the main authority in the process of laying down the constitution and codifying it and arranging it: to what extent will it fulfil its covenant and its promises and undertake its responsibility and its duty?

And in this discussion we shall study, in summary, the principles of the Islamic constitution — of its meaning and its import, its components and its privileges. And we only see it as obligatory to put it before the people, so that it is with knowledge and understanding and a correct conception of the Islamic constitution, so that it becomes a touchstone for it by which it tests the new constitution: whether it conforms to those qualities or not? And then: whether it deserves acceptance as a constitution for the Islamic state or not?

And when we say that Islam is comprehensive and all-embracing for all the branches of life and its fields, we do not mean that every partial issue and secondary case of these fields has had its ruling expressly stated in the Qur'an and the Prophetic Sunna or the texts of Islamic jurisprudence. Rather the meaning: that Islam has legislated in every branch of the branches of life and its fields basic principles, and laid down main rulings sufficient

[p. 324] for extracting all their particulars from them. And the constitution is like that: Islam has granted us concerning it essential directives and basic starting-points, fixed, not capable of change — even if all humanity agreed on issuing a decision contrary to them or clashing with them, that would not be [valid] for it at all. As for what is besides these principles, it has entrusted their matter to the people of insight among the Muslims, to choose them by consultation among them what they see as most useful for their interests and most fitting to their circumstances — on condition that it not contradict those basic principles in anything.

And many of the constitutional issues that are discussed when laying down the constitution for the Islamic state are of this kind, which does not obstruct Islam in the way of choosing them by consultation if it agreed with these basic principles — like the multiplicity of the legislative body and its unity, the official language of the state, and the administrative units into which the state will be divided, and the specification of the manner of its connection with the federal central government preserving the coalition of the state, and the number of ministers and members of the house of representatives, and the ideal way for the accounting and financial auditing of the state. These are matters for which Islam has not defined a special form; rather it has entrusted them to the collective consultation of the Umma; so whatever it chose in this regard would be permissible and capable of actual implementation from the Sharia side.

As for the main issues and basic principles concerning which Islam has taken a clear, decisive position, which will remain definitively fixed over the passage of ages and the succession of eras, without there being in it latitude for change or room for amendment — even if the whole people agreed upon it, let alone the members of parliament and the legislative body — these are what we regard as the components of the Islamic constitution, such that the Islamization of a constitution is not completed if it is empty of these matters. And here below we clarify them and their basis from the Noble Qur'an.

### The components of the Islamic constitution

**(1)** Supreme sovereignty and absolute dominion in this universe belong to God the Exalted.

And its basis is His words, the Exalted: **"Judgement belongs to God alone"** [Yūsuf, 12:40; and al-Anʿām, 6:57].

And it is the first clause with which the Islamic constitution opens, and it is what distinguishes it from the systems of

[p. 325] secular democracy which acknowledge for the people alone dominion and authority, and which permit for it legislation contrary to the rulings of God if the whole people or its majority agreed on it. As for Islam, it does not acknowledge supreme sovereignty except for God; and man rules over his fellow human beings under the shade of his vicegerency and his deputyship. God the Exalted said: **"Indeed, I will make upon the earth a vicegerent"** [al-Baqara, 2:30].

**(2)** No legislation is enacted, and no law is agreed upon, and no administrative measure is issued, contrary to the Book of God and the Prophetic Sunna; and it does not remain if it had actually been taken.

**"And whoever does not judge by what God has revealed — then it is those who are the disbelievers"** [al-Māʾida, 5:44].

**(3)** The ultimate goal and the original intent of the government: that it establish justice and fairness, and uproot strife (*fitna*) and corruption from their roots, and provide for the Muslim the opportunities of performing acts of worship, and undertake commanding right and forbidding wrong. God the Exalted said: **"[They are] those who, if We establish them in the land, establish prayer and give zakat and enjoin what is right and forbid what is wrong. And to God belongs the outcome of [all] matters"** [al-Ḥajj, 22:41].

And: **"And when you judge between people, judge with justice"** [al-Nisāʾ, 4:58].

And: **"And if it were not for God checking [some] people by means of others, the earth would have been corrupted"** [al-Baqara, 2:251].

**(4)** All the offices of the government and its properties are a trust in the hands of the rulers, and among the duty of the governmental bodies is that they render them to their [rightful] people. God the Exalted said: **"Indeed, God commands you to render trusts to whom they are due"** [al-Nisāʾ, 4:58].

**(5)** The government shall not be individually despotic; it shall only be consultative (*shūrawiyya*), and all its important affairs shall be administered by consultation from the people of the state and its inhabitants. God the Exalted said: **"And whose affair is [determined by] consultation among themselves"** [al-Shūrā, 42:38].

**(6) Unity shall be on the basis of Islamic brotherhood, and it is upon the state to end partisanship (*al-ʿaṣabiyya*) among the Muslims on the basis of geography, tribe, language, race and the like of that which contradicts the principles of the Sharia, and to strive seriously in forming the unity of the Umma and its consolidation, and to establish social justice among all its Muslim citizens.** God, glorified and exalted, said: **"The believers

[p. 326] are but brothers"** [al-Ḥujurāt, 49:10].

And: **"And made you peoples and tribes that you may know one another. Indeed, the most noble of you in the sight of God is the most righteous of you"** [al-Ḥujurāt, 49:13].

**(7)** It is stipulated for the head of state that he be a Muslim, truthful, trustworthy, bearing scholarly and practical abilities and qualifications. God the Exalted said: **"My covenant does not include the wrongdoers"** [al-Baqara, 2:124].

And: **"Indeed, God has chosen him over you and has increased him abundantly in knowledge and stature"** [al-Baqara, 2:247].

**(8)** The state guarantees to all its citizens the basic rights of the human being: that they be safe from aggression against them in life, honour or property; and it grants them the freedom of assembly for good purposes, and the freedom of constructive criticism. God, glorified and exalted, said: **"And do not kill the soul which God has forbidden, except by right"** [al-Isrāʾ, 17:33].

And: **"And do not consume one another's wealth unjustly"** [al-Baqara, 2:188].
And: **"O you who have believed, let not a people ridicule [another] people"** [al-Ḥujurāt, 49:11].
And: **"There is no compulsion in religion"** [al-Baqara, 2:256].
And: **"And let there be [arising] from you a nation inviting to [all that is] good"** [Āl ʿImrān, 3:104].
And: **"You are the best nation produced for mankind: you enjoin what is right and forbid what is wrong"** [Āl ʿImrān, 3:110].

**(9)** No one shall be sentenced to a punishment as long as his crime has not been established. God, glorified and exalted, said: **"O you who have believed, if there comes to you a disobedient one with information, investigate, lest you harm a people out of ignorance and become, over what you have done, regretful"** [al-Ḥujurāt, 49:6].

**(10)** The judiciary shall be free, in fulfilling the requirements of justice and equity, from any pressure and interference — except the law of the pure Sharia. God, glorified and exalted, said: **"O you who have believed, be persistently standing firm in justice, witnesses for God, even if it be against yourselves or parents and relatives"** [al-Nisāʾ, 4:135].

And: **"So judge between them by what God has revealed and do not follow their inclinations"** [al-Māʾida, 5:48].

[p. 327] **(11)** The state shall guarantee the provision of vital needs for the needy, the disabled and the bankrupt among the citizens, and supply their necessities; and it shall undertake the just distribution of collective wealth, which enables the whole people to benefit from it, and it shall not remain circulating among the rich among them. God the Exalted said: **"And what God restored to His Messenger from the people of the towns — it is for God and for the Messenger and for [his] near relatives and orphans and the needy and the stranded traveller — so that it will not be a perpetual distribution among the rich from among you"** [al-Ḥashr, 59:7].

And: **"And from their properties was [given] the right of the [needy] petitioner and the deprived"** [al-Dhāriyāt, 51:19].

**(12)** The government shall not confiscate the individual personal properties which their owners have owned by ways permitted in the Sharia. God the Exalted said: **"And do not consume one another's wealth unjustly"** [al-Baqara, 2:188].

**(13)** No tax shall be imposed on the citizens beyond their capacity, and they shall not be charged with any administrative matter that is not within their ability. God the Exalted said: **"And relieves them of their burden and the shackles which were upon them"** [al-Aʿrāf, 7:157].

And: **"God does not charge a soul except [with that within] its capacity"** [al-Baqara, 2:286].

**(14)** The government shall provide to all Muslim citizens the service of education and upbringing which enables them to learn the Noble Qur'an and to know the basic rulings of Islam, and then makes them live a good, simple life with the observance of the Islamic teachings and in their light; and likewise it shall make available to them the opportunity for excellence and mastery in the economic sciences also, so that they make the state self-sufficient by itself, not in need in that of anyone. God, glorified and exalted, said: **"And teach them the Book and wisdom"** [al-Baqara, 2:129].

**(15)** Non-Muslim citizens (except the apostates among them) shall basically obtain all the human rights which the Muslims among them obtain. God, glorified and exalted, said: **"But if he was from a people with whom you have a treaty — then a compensation payment presented to his family"** [al-Nisāʾ, 4:92].

And: **"O you who have believed, fulfil [all] contracts"** [al-Māʾida, 5:1].

**(16)** The state shall undertake the duty of jihad according to the Islamic rulings. God the Exalted said: **"And strive for God with the striving due to Him"** [al-Ḥajj, 22:78].

[p. 328] **(17)** The relationship of peaceableness and tolerance and kindness with non-Islamic states shall be strengthened — except what of them was hostile to Islam and the Muslims. God the Exalted said: **"God does not forbid you from those who do not fight you because of religion and do not expel you from your homes — from being righteous toward them and acting justly toward them. Indeed, God loves those who act justly"** [al-Mumtaḥana, 60:8].

**(18)** The state shall be committed to fulfilling the covenants and permissible treaties which it contracted and concluded with other states, or [to] announcing their being thrown back and their ending openly and publicly. God the Exalted said: **"Except for those with whom you made a treaty among the polytheists and then they have not been deficient toward you in anything or supported anyone against you; so complete for them their treaty until their term [has ended]"** [al-Tawba, 9:4].

And: **"If you [have reason to] fear from a people betrayal, throw [their treaty] back to them, [putting you] on equal terms"** [al-Anfāl, 8:58].

**(19)** A non-Muslim shall not be put in charge of an executive office connected with the secrets of the Muslims and their secrets related to the affairs of the government. God the Exalted said: **"O you who have believed, do not take as intimates those other than yourselves"** [Āl ʿImrān, 3:118].

**(20)** No change at all shall be permitted in the clauses of the constitution which have been taken from the Qur'an and the Prophetic Sunna directly. God the Exalted said: **"And the word of your Lord has been fulfilled in truth and in justice. None can alter His words"** [al-Anʿām, 6:115].

So these are the basic points without the observance of which it is not possible to apply to a constitution [the description] that it is Islamic. And we have contented ourselves, in their bases, with what came concerning them of the Qur'anic verses, and did not undertake to explain the Prophetic hadiths which clarify them and detail them more. Rather the first written constitutional document in the world was that which the Prophet ﷺ arranged in the wake of the Islamic government in Medina the Illuminated, which clarified in it the civil rights of the citizens of Medina, Muslims and non-Muslims among them; and it contains 47 clauses, and ʿAllāma Ibn Hishām (may God have mercy on him) brought it in his *Sīra*.

## Appendix B: The Islamic Clauses in the Constitution

[p. 329] As we presented in what has passed, there are many political issues on which Islam has decided a definitive, decisive ruling, from which it is not permitted for the Umma to deviate, nor to oppose it or contradict it. And to guarantee the compliance with them and their application at the level of the state, the constitution-making for the state must pass through the four steps which we shall clarify in this discussion of ours.

### The first step

That it make the Objectives Resolution which the legislative assembly adopted in the year 1951 [sic; 1949] a necessary part, not separable from the constitution in any case; because it is what defines the course of the basic policy of the state and specifies the direction of its main plan — and it acknowledged in it that the true rule belongs to God, glorified and exalted, and that the elected representatives of the people will undertake using their authority within the framework of those limits and the commitment to those restrictions which the rulings of the Qur'an and the Sunna impose upon them. And let it be provided in the constitution itself that the law or the governmental measure which contradicts the points adopted in that resolution will be subject to review and appeal in the Supreme Court of the state.

### The second step

The second stage is of gravity and standing in the Islamization of the constitution, namely: specifying the sound principles and foundations for legislation and law-making in the state; and let the constitution guarantee non-legislation of what contradicts a ruling of the rulings of the Qur'an and the Sunna, and likewise that no administrative measure contrary to the Qur'an and the Sunna be taken; and that what contradicts them of the laws of the state will be disposed of until they agree with the rulings of the Sharia.

And let the following matters and points be noted in that with precision and mastery:

**(a)** In view of the express statement which the constitution includes of undertaking disposal and change

[p. 330] in the laws which contradict the Sharia, let there be defined in the constitution itself an appropriate period sufficient for the process of Islamizing the laws in the new constitution — and it should not exceed five years in any case; and I am confident that it suffices for codifying the Islamic laws if one worked in it with seriousness and sincerity.

**(b)** And in this clause of the constitution, let both the Qur'an and the Sunna be stated clearly and explicitly; and it is not sufficient in that with the word "Islam" or "the Qur'an" alone — and that is in acceptance of and faith in the Prophetic Sunna as a source of legislation and a basis of law.

**(c)** And so that the benefit of this clause is completed, it is necessary to state in the constitution that the house of representatives, if it adopted a law contrary to the Qur'an and the Sunna, it is within the scope of change by challenge and appeal in the Supreme Court.

**(d)** It is necessary to specify the way which is followed in ruling on a law as being contrary to the rulings of the Qur'an and the Sunna; and for this purpose let it be provided in the constitution what guarantees entrusting this work to the scholars of the Sharia who learned the sciences of the Qur'an and the Sunna and studied them in a regular study. And the best way for that is that a special judicial body belonging to the Supreme Court be designated, comprising a judge from its judges and five (or six) proficient scholars.

**(e)** And concerning the designation of the committee which undertakes reviewing the laws and making them in agreement with the Sharia, let the constitution provide with precision and careful thought for the qualities of the members of this committee which will perform the task of codifying the Islamic laws.

And in view of these matters, we see that the clause concerned with legislation should be in the constitution as follows:

**(1)** No law shall be legislated contrary to the Qur'an and the Sunna; and if there were in the constitution of the country a law contrary to them, it shall be disposed of and changed according to what comes in the following paragraph.

**(2)** And to undertake the task mentioned in the first paragraph, the head of state shall form, within six months from the day of implementing the constitution, a committee called "the Islamic Law Committee", in which there shall be representatives of all the Muslim Islamic sects present in the state.

[p. 331] **(3)** Two thirds of the members of this committee shall be of the scholars who are characterized by the following qualities:

(a) That he has occupied the office of fatwa for a period of ten years at least in one of the well-known religious institutes or universities.

(b) Or he has remained the reference of fatwa in a region of the regions of the Muslims for a period of ten years at least.

(c) Or he has worked as a Sharia judge in one of the official Sharia judicial departments.

(d) Or he has undertaken teaching the subjects of the commentary on the Noble Qur'an and the Prophetic hadith or jurisprudence for a period of ten years at least in an institute or university among the religious institutes and universities.

**(4)** And the remaining third of the members of the Islamic Law Committee shall be of legal specialists who:

(a) Have occupied the office of judge in the High Court or the two High Courts in succession for a period of five years at least.

(b) Or have remained a lawyer for the High Court or the two High Courts in succession for a period of fifteen years.

**(5)** And the best arrangement for this appears to be that the head of state, before appointing them, request from all the religious and political parties in the state the names of members of this committee by way of proposal; so whatever of the names was shared among all the proposals, he shall not leave it — except if the conditions mentioned in paragraphs 3 and 4 were not present in it.

**(6)** The Islamic Law Committee shall undertake the fitting codification of the Islamic rulings within five years from the day of its establishment, and shall present its report — temporary or final — to the national parliament within six months from the day of its receiving it, so that parliament undertakes implementing it as law.

**(7)** If objections were raised and remarks were brought against the laws which the legislative assembly legislated, that they are contrary to the rulings of the Qur'an and the Sunna, then the decisive word and the final

[p. 332] ruling in it shall be for the judicial body belonging to the Supreme Court, which the head of state designates for this purpose, and at least five of its members shall be from the scholars of the Sharia.

**(8)** And for the judicial body belonging to the Supreme Court mentioned in paragraph 7, there shall only be designated of the scholars those in whom the qualities mentioned in paragraph 3 are present; and all the regulations which I proposed to apply to the other judges shall apply to them also.

### Explanatory points for this clause

**(1)** With regard to applying this clause to the personal laws of the sects among the Muslim Islamic sects, what counts in it is the interpretation which that particular sect accepts.

**(2)** No weight shall be given to any explanation and clarification of the Qur'an and the Prophetic Sunna contrary to what all the schools of jurisprudence agreed upon; so that explanation shall be regarded as abrogated, cancelled to the extent of the mentioned contradiction.

And we see that the condition of restriction by the Qur'an and the Sunna in the work of legislation only bears fruit and produces good if it is mentioned in the constitution according to the details that have passed.

### The third step in Islamizing the constitution

It is: that the constitution provide for stipulating Islam for each of the head of state and the prime minister.

### The fourth step

It is: that the work of laying down the constitution put before its eyes those twenty-two points which 31 great scholars arranged and agreed upon, among them those belonging to every Muslim Islamic sect. So let the new constitution be founded on those principles and starting-points, and let whatever contradicts them of the clauses of the constitution be regarded as abrogated and void. And here below we transmit to you those principles, Arabized.

[p. 333]

### The basic principles of the Islamic state

The Islamic constitution must provide in it for the following principles:

**(1)** God, glorified and exalted, is the true Ruler of the universe — creatively and legislatively.

**(2)** The law of the state shall be built on the Book and the Sunna, and no law shall be enacted, and no administrative measure ordered, contrary to the Book and the Sunna.

(Explanatory note)

If laws of this kind were actually in force in the state, then it is necessary to state in the constitution that they will be cancelled, or that the Sharia will be applied gradually within a defined period.

**(3)** The state shall not be built on a geographical, racial or linguistic conception; it shall only be built on the principles and goals that emanate from the constitution of Islamic life.

**(4)** Among the duty of the Islamic state is that it command what is right and establish it, and forbid what is wrong and remove it, and establish the Islamic rites and raise them high, and provide the service of basic religious education for every Muslim Islamic sect according to their schools.

**(5)** Just as among its duty is to strengthen the bonds of unity and brotherhood among all the Muslims of the world, and to block all the ways which call and incite to racial or regional partisanship or other material differences among the Muslims of the state — and that is for preserving Islamic unity and its consolidation.

**(6)** The state shall provide the basic human needs — of food, housing, clothing, medical care and education — for every disabled citizen incapable of earning a livelihood, or who has actually become temporarily incapable of it because of unemployment or illness or other than them of causes, regardless of his religion and his race and the like of that.

**(7)** The citizens of the state shall obtain all the rights which the Sharia has granted them — of the protection of life, property and honour, and freedom of religion and belief, and of worship, and of the person, and freedom of opinion,

[p. 334] and of movement, and of assembly, and of earning a livelihood, and equality in social advancement, and the right to benefit from charitable institutions — all of that within the framework of the law.

**(8)** No right of these mentioned civil rights shall be stripped from any citizen except if it is legitimate from the perspective of Islamic law; and no one shall be punished on a charge of crime as long as he has not been granted an opportunity to deny it, and as long as a court has not ruled against him by it.

**(9)** The accepted Muslim Islamic sects shall be, within the limits of the law, in complete freedom in practising their religious affairs; just as they shall have the right to teach their children their religious study, and to spread their ideas and their beliefs freely. And they shall be judged in their personal affairs and their disputes according to their school of jurisprudence; and it shall be more fitting to provide a judge from among those belonging to that sect.

**(10)** Just as non-Muslim citizens shall obtain, within the limits of the law, the freedom of belief, worship, civilization and culture, and religious education; and they shall have the right to request that they be judged in their personal affairs according to their religious law or what has prevailed among them of rites and traditions.

**(11)** It is obligatory to fulfil the Sharia covenants which the state has contracted with the non-Muslim citizens, and they shall have all the civil rights mentioned in clause 7, equally between them and the Muslims.

**(12)** It is stipulated for the head of state that he be a male Muslim, trusted in his trustworthiness and his competence and the correctness of his opinion by the masses or their elected representatives.

**(13)** The head of state is the one who is responsible for the administration of the kingdom, and it is permitted for him to delegate something of his authorities to an individual or a group.

**(14)** The government of the head of state shall not be an individual, despotic government; rather it shall be built on the basis of shūrā, in the sense that he undertakes his works and performs his responsibilities by consultation with the members of the government and the representatives of the people.

**(15)** The head of state shall never have the right of suspending the constitution, wholly or partially, nor of administering

[p. 335] the government without shūrā.

**(16)** The group which undertakes the authority of choosing the president shall have the authority to remove him also.

**(17)** The head of state shall be in civil rights like the generality of the Muslims, and shall be subject to the sovereignty of law.

**(18)** There shall apply to each of the officials and members of the government and the generality of civilians a unified law and regulation, which the courts shall implement on both groups.

**(19)** The judicial section shall be independent and entirely free from the administrative section, so that the courts are not affected, in performing their duties and fulfilling their responsibilities, by any pressure or governmental influence.

**(20)** The spread of the ideas and theories destructive of the basic principles of the Islamic state and its values, its principles and its starting-points shall be prohibited — and likewise the call to them — with a categorical prohibition.

**(21)** The various provinces of the state and its sectors shall be treated as administrative units of the unified state; and their status shall not be as sectarian units on the basis of racial, linguistic or tribal [identity]. They are only sectors into which the state has been divided administratively for the purpose of facilitation in administering the helm of government; and it is permitted that administrative authorities be delegated to them in subordination to the sovereignty of the centre; and it is not their right to secede from the centre at all.

**(22)** No weight shall be given to any explanation and clarification of the constitution contrary to the Book and the Sunna.
