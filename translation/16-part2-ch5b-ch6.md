
[p. 299] Perhaps human history has not seen — over its whole extent — an example of goodwill and good companionship more splendid and more majestic than what came from the Prophet ﷺ when God enabled him, on the day of the conquest of Mecca, over his enemies who were thirsty for his blood, whose fierce enmities had stretched for a long period in which they harmed him in every way of harm possible for them. But he did not treat them harshly and did not take revenge from them; rather it was nothing from him except that he said to them: "No blame upon you today; you are the freed ones (*al-ṭulaqāʾ*)."

These events teach us that what is hateful is not the unbelievers themselves, but their false beliefs and their mischief (*fitna*). As for showing them goodwill and sincere concern on the basis of humanity, that is desirable in the Sharia. It is from this very principle of sincere concern for them that the command to invite them to Islam springs; and jihad likewise has no aim other than sincere concern for all humanity — preserving and protecting it from the corruption and strife that unbelief and tyranny bring upon it.

### Cooperation in good works

Above all that, Islam permits partnership and cooperation with the unbelievers in matters of good, righteousness and keeping ties — indeed urges it — as an explicit, clear Qur'anic text came concerning it: **"And cooperate in righteousness and piety, but do not cooperate in sin and aggression"** [al-Māʾida, 5:2].

Let it be known that this portion of the verse came in the context of [forbidding] aggression against the unbelievers; and the verse in its entirety is as follows: **"And do not let the hatred of a people for having obstructed you from al-Masjid al-Ḥarām lead you to transgress. Cooperate in righteousness and piety, but do not cooperate in sin and aggression. Fear God; indeed, God is severe in penalty"** [al-Māʾida, 5:2].

So it includes cooperation with the unbelievers too in righteousness and good works. From this, if the unbelievers put forward a project which aims at the interest of humanity, and in which there is not what conflicts with the interest of Islam and the Muslims, then it is permissible — indeed recommended — for the Muslims to contribute to it and take part in accomplishing it.

There are found in the biography of the Prophet ﷺ many examples of the relationship of partnership and participation with the unbelievers in matters of good, repelling evil and deterring from injustice — the most prominent of them the pact which the Prophet ﷺ contracted

[p. 300] in participation with the unbelievers, by the name "Ḥilf al-Fuḍūl" (the Pact of the Virtuous). Here are its details in what follows:

### Ḥilf al-Fuḍūl (the Pact of the Virtuous)

The pacts of mutual cooperation took place in the Jāhiliyya on the basis of the tribe, in the sense that a person was committed to helping whoever belonged to his tribe, whether in the right or the wrong, just or unjust, wrongdoer or wronged. In all these cases a person was bound to support his tribesman, even if he was upon clear injustice and evident aggression.

Ḥilf al-Fuḍūl was the noblest pact ever heard of and the most honourable among the Arabs. The first to speak of it and call to it was al-Zubayr ibn ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib. Its cause was that a man of Zubayd came to Mecca with goods, and al-ʿĀṣ ibn Wāʾil bought them from him; and he had power and nobility in Mecca, so he withheld his right from him. So the Zubaydī sought help against him from the allies: ʿAbd al-Dār, Makhzūm, Jumaḥ, Sahm and ʿAdī ibn Kaʿb; but they refused to help him against al-ʿĀṣ ibn Wāʾil and rebuked him — that is, they repelled him. When the Zubaydī saw this ill treatment, he went up on Abū Qubays at sunrise, while Quraysh were in their gatherings around the Kaʿba, and cried out at the top of his voice:

> *O family of Fihr, for one wronged in his goods,*
> *in the heart of Mecca, far from home and kin…*

So al-Zubayr ibn ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib stood up for that and said: This cannot be left unanswered. At any rate, in view of the aforementioned hadith in which the Prophet ﷺ praised Ḥilf al-Fuḍūl and showed his resolve to answer [it] if he were called to it today, ʿAllāma al-Suhaylī (may God have mercy on him) and others of the jurists held that it is permissible for the Muslims — indeed recommended — that they enter with non-Muslims into a covenant aiming at helping the wronged and repelling injustice and the like of that of the good purposes beneficial to humanity.

### Jihad

It has become clear from the detail that has passed that the nature of the Islamic state's relationship with non-Muslims is determined by the conduct of the unbelievers, as God the Exalted said: **"So as long as they are upright toward you, be upright toward them"** [al-Tawba, 9:7].

But the elements which form a real threat to Islam and the Muslims, which have launched all their forces in hostility against them and made them a target for their aggression and persecution; and which try to obstruct the course of the Islamic call by putting obstacles in its way; or whose habit and occupation have become corruption and destruction against humanity — the Islamic government founded on straight foundations does not know, to deter them from their errors, any option except jihad and struggle, armed and unarmed.

So from it is known the first purpose of fighting, namely that it be for defending Islam or the Islamic state if the unbelievers attacked it. The second purpose of fighting in the light of the Noble Qur'an is: rescuing the Muslims who face waves of injustice and persecution in an unbelieving state in which they reside. God — mighty is the Speaker — said: **"And what is [the matter] with you that you fight not in the cause of God and [for] the oppressed among men, women and children who say, 'Our Lord, take us out of this city of oppressive people and appoint for us from Yourself a protector and appoint for us from Yourself a helper'?"** [al-Nisāʾ, 4:75].

The third aim which Islam aims at behind the legitimacy of fighting is: curbing *fitna* (persecution/oppression). The ruling of jihad for curbing *fitna* is not confined to cases where those persecuted are Muslims; rather whenever the unbelievers set up in the world a tyrannical, oppressive, despotic system, and began to bind God's servants in it with their man-made rulings and invented laws, the Islamic Sharia commands its followers to fight them in such a case, to rescue humanity from their injustice by breaking their power and ending their dominance, and striving to establish the just divine order in its place. This is what we mean to include among the aims which Islam seeks behind the legitimacy of fighting, and it is what is meant in His words, the Exalted: **"And fight them until there is no fitna and [until] the religion, all of it, is for God. If they cease — then indeed, God is Seeing of what they do"** [al-Anfāl, 8:39].

It is what Ribʿī ibn ʿĀmir, may God be pleased with him, expressed in the council of Rustam — commander of the army of Iran — when he asked him about what had brought them to Iran; he answered him, saying: "God has sent us to bring out whom He wills from the worship of servants to the worship of God, and from the narrowness of this world to its breadth, and from the tyranny of religions to the justice of Islam."¹

Whoever reviews the system of the tyrannical Chosroes and becomes acquainted with the forms of his enslaving of the subjects will grasp precisely how true these words of Ribʿī were.

At any rate, these are several purposes for whose realization jihad was legislated in Islam; and this brief discussion has no room for further detail on them. Islam, by complete contrast, first established that fighting must be for a legitimate cause and a good purpose — which made it a war with honour and dignity, and protected it from being an instrument of enslavement and colonization and the ownership of lands and control over wealth, on the one hand. ## Chapter Six: On Removing the Imam and Overthrowing the Government

### Introduction

[p. 305] Many Western writers have expressed their opinion that Islam does not permit following any way to topple the government and remove it once it has been formed and established. Perhaps its source is nothing but the superficial, hasty look at the Islamic rulings which emphasize the prohibition of rebellion (*al-khurūj*) against the imam and forbid it categorically. But it is in reality a pure error which has no basis at all from argument or reason. Rather, just as Islam possesses within its folds safe ways for appointing the imam and designating the caliph, so too its teachings cover how to remove and depose him by calm, peaceful ways.

### 1 – Peaceful, safe removal

The following are the peaceful grounds on which the imam is removed from office under the Sharia:

**(1)** If he removed himself — that is, he submitted the resignation and showed his inability to continue the work as caliph and imam; for the Sharia judges in this form that he is removed, as is apparent.

**(2)** That something befall him which prevents him from performing the functions of the imam — as if madness overtook him, or illness struck him which prevented him from undertaking even his ordinary affairs and his daily occupations. So it is self-evident that his removal is judged in this form also.

**(3)** If he committed something of the matters of transgression (*fisq*), like drinking wine and taking bribes and imposing non-Sharia taxes on the subjects, and other than that of the matters of transgression, including injustice and tyranny in rule as well. Its ruling is that he deserves removal for his committing these matters; but fighting him and armed rebellion against him are not yet permissible. This means that if the Muslims are able to remove him by a safe way, they are obliged to do so, as al-Ḥāfiẓ Ibn Ḥajar (may God have mercy on him) said: "What the scholars hold concerning the commanders

[p. 306] of tyranny is that if he is able to depose him without strife (*fitna*) and without injustice, it is obligatory; otherwise the obligation is patience."¹

The meaning of patience here: the prohibition of raising weapons against him and removing him by acts of armed violence; this does not preclude efforts by safe, peaceful means — for example, the matter could be referred to the shūrā council or the court to determine whether the transgressions he has committed warrant his removal; and if they find that they do, they would remove him.

**(4)** And if there was not found any of these three matters, but his policies and his plans do not appear in agreement with the interests of the state, such that aversion occurs towards him among the people — does he deserve by this his removal or not? I did not find its ruling expressly stated in the words of the scholars, neither in negation nor in affirmation. What seems most correct to me, in view of the general rules, is that there would be no harm if the shūrā which chose him and put him in charge of the imamate removed him in such circumstances by peaceful constitutional means. It is supported by the hadith of Anas ibn Mālik, may God be pleased with him, that the Messenger of God ﷺ cursed three, among them: "A man who led a people [in prayer] while they disliked him."²

Let it be known that there is talk about the chain of this hadith; but the route by which al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī narrated it as mursal is sound, just as it is soundly narrated as mawqūf from ʿAmr ibn al-Ḥārith ibn al-Muṣṭaliq, may God be pleased with him; and al-Tirmidhī (may God have mercy on him) himself cited it after this hadith.

So the hadith indicated that it is not commendable for a person to insist on the imamate of Muslims who dislike him; so if it were possible to replace him with one better than him and more righteous, doing so would accord with the import of this Prophetic hadith, upon its owner be blessing and peace.

Also, ʿAwf ibn Mālik narrated from the Messenger of God ﷺ; he said: "The best of your imams are those whom you love and who love you, and who pray for you and you pray for them; and the worst of your imams are those whom you hate and who hate you, and whom you curse and who curse you."³

Proceeding from this, if the Muslims wanted to lay down the constitution of the state on Islamic foundations, it is fitting that they provide in it for a clause concerned with defining the ways and mechanisms that will be adopted for his deposition in the like of these situations and states.

**(4)** [sic] It has prevailed in most states of the world today that the headship of the state — whether the head of state or the prime minister occupies it — is held for a fixed term, and its holder leaves office automatically merely by the passing of that period, so that another president fills his place. Although no fixed term existed for the caliph or chosen imam in Islamic history, Islam — as we said more than once — has granted its rulings which it legislated in the sector of politics a flexibility with which the needs of this age and its requirements are accommodated; for we do not find any ruling in the Qur'an or the Prophetic Sunna which contradicts defining a period for the imamate and the command. So if the shūrā which chooses and appoints the imam sets a fixed term for him, there is no harm in that from the Sharia point of view. But the term should be long enough for the president to carry out his policies and plans effectively. If this is accepted, it would be the fourth way for the removal of the imam, summarized in that he is removed by the passing of the period of the imamate defined for him.

### Rebellion against the imam [undertaking acts of armed violence against the imam]

It becomes clear from the teachings of the Prophet ﷺ and his directives that he regarded civil wars in the Islamic state as among the worst of matters and the most heinous of wrongs. It is the matter which he ﷺ strongly emphasized

Lo, do not return after me as misguided ones [or: unbelievers], some of you striking the necks of others. Lo, let the present inform the absent; for perhaps some of those whom it reaches will be more retentive of it than some of those who heard it." Then he said: "Lo, have I conveyed?" twice.¹

Hence we see that the Islamic Sharia has sometimes tolerated grave wrongs merely for protecting the Muslims from plunging into civil wars. The Prophet ﷺ repeatedly forbade, with a categorical prohibition, in different ways and various styles, armed rebellion against the imam or the caliph whose rule has been established and whose authority is in place — so that ferocious wars do not break out and bloody battles do not erupt among the Muslims. There passed before us the hadith of ʿAwf ibn Mālik al-Ashjaʿī in which came: "Lo, whoever has a governor appointed over him, and he sees him do something of disobedience to God, let him dislike what he does of disobedience to God, and let him not withdraw a hand from obedience."²

It is even reported from Ḥudhayfa ibn al-Yamān, may God be pleased with him, that the Prophet ﷺ said once, in what he said about the trials (*fitan*) of the end of time: "And there will stand among them men whose hearts are the hearts of devils in the bodies of humans." **The second:** the jurists have agreed altogether that rebellion, in all forms of its permissibility, requires two things: power and strength (*al-qudra wa-l-manʿa*), and that it not cause a harm greater than the harm of this imam's remaining. Here too opinions may differ. This is just as Imam Abū Ḥanīfa (may God have mercy on him), even if he saw rebellion as permissible in itself, nevertheless did not take part in it, giving as reason: "If one man rose for it, he would be killed, and the people's affair would not be set right. But if he found righteous helpers against it, and a man to lead them who is trusted in the religion of God… And this is an obligation which is not like the obligations which a man undertakes alone; and this, whenever the man undertook it alone, he would shed his blood and expose himself to being killed; so I fear that he would help in killing himself."¹

At any rate, Imam Abū Ḥanīfa and al-Ḥasan ibn Ṣāliḥ ibn Ḥayy (may God have mercy on them both) used to permit armed rebellion against the transgressing imams who administer the helm of government with what entails contempt of the Sharia; but the jurists after them — including the followers of Ḥanafī jurisprudence also — agreed, in the light of their seasoned experiences in this field, that acts of armed violence like this usually do not lead, for the Umma, to a better outcome; so it is better that one be careful of it and avoid it — as al-Ḥāfiẓ Ibn Ḥajar (may God have mercy on him) said in the biography of al-Ḥasan ibn Ṣāliḥ (may God have mercy on him):

"[Al-Ḥasan ibn Ṣāliḥ] used to see the sword — meaning rebellion against the imams of tyranny; and this is an old school of the predecessors; but the matter settled on abandoning that, because they saw that it led to what is more severe than it. For in the event of al-Ḥarra and the event of Ibn al-Ashʿath and others than them there is an admonition for whoever reflects… And al-Ḥasan, with that, did not rebel against anyone."²

Al-Ḥāfiẓ (may God have mercy on him) himself said, in the explanation of one of the hadiths narrated from Ibn ʿAbbās,

[p. 313] may God be pleased with him: "Ibn Baṭṭāl said: In the hadith is an argument for abandoning rebellion against the sultan even if he is tyrannical. They made no exception except where open unbelief issued from the sultan; then obeying him in that is not permissible; rather fighting him is obligatory for whoever is able to."¹

But Imam Abū Bakr al-Jaṣṣāṣ (may God have mercy on him), building on the action of Imam Abū Ḥanīfa (may God have mercy on him), held, in the commentary on the noble verse **"My covenant does not include the wrongdoers"** [al-Baqara, 2:124], that it is permissible to rebel against the imams of tyranny even if this involves bloodshed and strife — on condition that there be ability for it, and that it not lead to a more dangerous harm and a greater corruption. Indeed he held that the government of transgressors has no legitimacy and their rulings are not to be implemented.

But what appears to me is that the later jurists chose the same position which passed in the aforementioned text of *Fatḥ al-Bārī*. ʿAllāma al-Sarakhsī (may God have mercy on him) said: "And by this wording argues whoever claims that the ruler is removed by tyranny; and this is not our school, and we have explained that in what we dictated of the commentary on *al-Ziyādāt* 'in the chapter on arbitration'."²

### Political movements

There is another issue worth mentioning and studying in this discussion of ours, namely: when rebellion against the imam is not permissible, is there a safe way that Islam permits to bring down a government and replace it with another, to meet the people's demands and recover their rights? How does Islam look at the political movements which are adopted in contemporary political circles to achieve this purpose?

It is a fact that Western ideas and theories have become — for centuries — the first bricks upon which we Muslims found the structure of our intellectual life and the edifice

[p. 314] of our civilizational existence in all branches of life in general, and politics among them in particular. Hence many political practices imported from the West have come to be regarded as virtually indispensable to political life, regardless of whether they are sound or corrupt. Among these practices: the methods of popular protest prevailing widely, like strikes and demonstrations accompanied by sabotage and destruction, and other than that of the ways people tread to pressure the government and carry it to yield and respond to their demands.

Concerning the ruling on these political practices from a Sharia perspective, we say: some of them are definitively forbidden, not permissible in the Sharia at all and absolutely — like a hunger strike to the point of starving oneself to death; and likewise any other method that leads to violating the sanctity of someone's blood or property or honour, or harming public governmental property, or damaging the funds of the state. Aggression against the properties of the state and its funds is among the gravest of sins, one for which it is extremely hard to make amends or escape punishment; by virtue of its not being a special personal property of the rulers only — rather in it is a right for all the citizens of the state and its inhabitants. So toying with it and inflicting any harm on it means injuring the right of the whole people. Since it relates to the rights of servants, release from it is not possible except by being absolved by all the holders of rights — and how is a person to be absolved, or to seek release, from all the inhabitants of the state?! So his shoulder then remains weighed down with the burden of this great, enormous disobedience.

As for the ruling on the general strike, we say: if someone appeals to the people and asks them to express their anger and resentment at a government measure by closing their businesses for a time, and then leaves them free to respond to this request or not — there is no harm in the Sharia in the mere request or in responding to it, provided the people are not coerced or compelled. For a strike like this is permissible in itself in the Sharia as a permissible measure for attaining the permissible goal — on condition that there be excepted in it what is indispensable for human beings, like medical care and others, and especially in emergencies.

But it has become an inseparable part of the strike that whoever appeals for it and calls to it does not content himself

[p. 315] with appealing and calling; rather the situation has deteriorated to the point that they compel the people to contribute to their strike and force them to suspend their works and close their commercial shops, and rain stones on whoever dared to take his car out into the street, and block the streets and public roads, and use violence against whoever does not contribute to the strike — or at least vent their fury on them. It is not hidden that all these works are definitively forbidden in the Sharia; besides that they cause several other evils as well: for the poor worker who earns his daily food is deprived, he and his family, of their provision; and the sick one who suffers from the severity of his illness — loss of treatment increases him in suffering and hardship; and some of them meet their death because of their inability to obtain treatment.

What one never ceases to be astonished and amazed at in this regard is: the double standard of democracy and its treatment with two faces: for it claims to support freedom of expression and opinion on one side, while denying those who do not join the strike any right to these freedoms, and forcing them to take part whether they like it or not. So this is among what clashes with the principles of Islam and contradicts moral values and principles, and specifically the principle of respecting freedom of opinion and thought.

Rarely is a strike free of these evils and harms in this age of ours; and that a strike of a safe, peaceful kind should occur in this atmosphere of ours, whose organizers act with decency and honour, leaving to the people the way to respond to their calls or not — that is extremely rare.

The permissible act, if it is taken as a means to forbidden things and a way to committing what is not permissible in the Sharia, then that permissible thing should be forbidden in keeping with the principle of blocking the means (*sadd al-dharāʾiʿ*). From this, the measure of the strike which produces destruction and sabotage, and the disturbance of security and peace, and obstructing people from continuing their works, does not fall within the measures permitted in the Sharia.

Since it is established that politics is not intended in itself — rather the original intent and the ultimate goal is attaining the pleasure of God, glorified and exalted — therefore let us take the ways and methods which our Lord, majestic and exalted, is pleased with, of what does not contradict the Sharia; otherwise there is no meaning to attempting

[p. 316] to implement Islam by way of contradicting its rulings one after the other.

Marches and demonstrations — their ruling likewise is that they are permissible in themselves if they do not cause severe harm to the people; but their nature, like the strike, rarely is free also of acts of violence, sabotage and causing problems for the people; and it is apparent that the saying of their permissibility from this aspect is not possible.

After this detail, the question which we put forward at the beginning of the discussion repeats itself here: is there a way other than them for pressuring the government and obtaining rights from it?

So in answer to it we say: the truth is that the Sharia has guided us to a way and taught us a principle — if we took it with regard, the tyrannical governments would surrender to it and the despotic authorities would submit to it — namely what the Prophet ﷺ expressed by his saying: "No obedience to a creature in disobedience to the Creator."

If the people once embraced it, kept it always before their eyes, and refused as one to carry out any government command or ruling that contradicts the Sharia — then by God tell me: what trick would the government have left, what arrow in its quiver?! Suppose that all the judges working in the courts announced their refusal to work as long as it is not permitted for them to rule by the rulings of the Sharia; and the lawyers, all of them, if they determined their resolve on not appearing before the courts in the capacity of advocacy as long as its laws are not dyed with a religious dye; and the employees of the banks, if they all refrained from providing their services in them, and the beneficiaries of them — the depositors — abstained, to the last of them, from depositing funds in them; and all the merchants from using their financing facilities as long as their system is not purified of usury — if all of these undertook this strike built on the basis of the aforementioned Sharia rule [no obedience to a creature in disobedience to the Creator], they would find it more useful for their purpose and more damaging to the governmental bodies — enough to bring them at once to yield to their demands and needs.

So this is the Sharia method for showing protest against the government; except that — since it needs something of sacrifice — people find it unpalatable and the public do not find it easy. For it is very easy for a person to shout "Long live Islam" and raise slogans on its behalf while

[p. 317] freely engaging in usurious transactions; and likewise it is easy — how easy it is! — that the judge and the lawyer be occupied with undertaking their professions according to man-made laws even if clashing with the rulings of the Sharia, and with that they contribute to the demonstrations in the right of Islam and shout "Long live Islam". As for using these professions and functions of theirs which they practise — of the judiciary, advocacy, banking and others — as a weapon to attain the goal of implementing Islam, it needs something of resolve and sacrifice; and how much we need them and how much they are lacking among us!

We grant that this approach will only work if the majority of the people is prepared for it, or at least a large number of them for whom the government reckons and gives weight, such that it suffices for pressuring it. It is known that preparing this amount is not completed except after directing the people and making it aware This requires organized planning by the political parties and groups — provided they first open their minds to this idea and wholeheartedly accept this principle — and then that they prepare a programme and planning a project aiming at the awareness of the people and its education in this field, beginning from the educational institutions and ending with the public mass meetings. So if this campaign undertook acquainting people with the harms of the jobs in which they are compelled to cooperate in forbidden matters, and their grasping the extent of the value of the lawful and its gravity — so that it made the majority of the people averse to them and dislike them, and then prepared to abandon them even at the cost of material sacrifice and of living a less comfortable and luxurious life than it was before — then this campaign would obtain a dazzling success and an overwhelming victory, and would pave the way for implementing the Sharia more than any strike or demonstration.

This is just as the army has often suspended or abrogated the constitution in Pakistan and other states, and succeeded because each time some judges of the higher courts lent its action legal legitimacy. But some of them refused to endorse any unconstitutional measure; and though they lost their posts and material benefits, they won the trust, esteem and respect of the people and praise for their role — so much so that people felt the other judges should have followed their example, and were certain that it would not have been within the power of

[p. 318] anyone to cancel the constitution if they all refused to document it legitimately. This actually happened when a large number of the judges of the Supreme Court in Pakistan agreed on opposing what General Pervez Musharraf undertook of cancelling the constitution; so the world saw, and witnessed with its own eyes, that success in the end was not except for those judges, not for the tyrannical despot.

As we presented in what has passed, the basic constitution of the Islamic state and its primary sources of legislation are nothing but the Qur'an and the Prophetic Sunna — and the constitution of Pakistan has provided for that, praise be to God. So if the motives for preserving them were aroused in the people, and the sentiments of protecting them were stirred in it as they were against Pervez Musharraf, this principle [no obedience to a creature in disobedience to the Creator] would play its role greatly, as a splendid and highly effective method of safe, peaceful protest.

### Political movements and the aspect of self-reform

This point too is of gravity and standing in the subject: that Islam does not aspire, from political efforts and movements, to obtaining authority or possessing power over lands and servants; rather the ultimate goal of them in Islam is: reforming the system of government, seeking the pleasure of God, glorified and exalted, by making it in agreement with His Sharia, and administering it according to what He loves and is pleased with. Since it is not necessary for achieving this goal that the holders of these efforts and movements themselves reach the seats of authority, there is no need, from the Sharia point of view, for them to take power themselves if the goal can be achieved by reforming those already in power, or through others — groups or individuals.

The first thing stipulated for this is that these workers in the field of Islamic politics belonging to its groups be sincere of intention, sound of disposition, free of the taints of love of status and position and seeking command and authority.
