
[p. 299] And perhaps human history has not seen — over its whole extent — an example of goodwill and good companionship more splendid and more majestic than what came from the Prophet ﷺ when God enabled him, on the day of the conquest of Mecca, over his enemies who were thirsty for his blood, whose fierce enmities had stretched for a long period in which they harmed him in every way of harm possible for them. But he did not treat them harshly and did not take revenge from them; rather it was nothing from him except that he said to them: "No blame upon you today; you are the freed ones (*al-ṭulaqāʾ*)."

So it is extracted from within these events that the hated one is not the unbelievers in themselves, but their false beliefs and their strife (*fitna*). As for showing them goodwill and counsel on the way of humanity, it is sought in the Sharia. And from the principle of counselling them and keeping ties with them is the command to call them to Islam; just as jihad does not strive behind it, and does not aim by undertaking it, except at counsel for humanity altogether — preserving it and protecting it from the corruption and the strife which unbelief and tyranny drag towards it.

### Cooperation in good works

And above all that, Islam permits partnership and cooperation with the unbelievers in matters of good, righteousness and keeping ties — indeed urges it — as an explicit, clear Qur'anic text came concerning it: **"And cooperate in righteousness and piety, but do not cooperate in sin and aggression"** [al-Māʾida, 5:2].

And let it be known that this portion of the verse came in the context of aggression against the unbelievers; and the verse in its entirety is as follows: **"And do not let the hatred of a people for having obstructed you from al-Masjid al-Ḥarām lead you to transgress. And cooperate in righteousness and piety, but do not cooperate in sin and aggression. And fear God; indeed, God is severe in penalty"** [al-Māʾida, 5:2].

So it included cooperation with the unbelievers too in righteousness and good works. And from this, if the unbelievers put forward a project which aims at the interest of humanity, and in which there is not what conflicts with the interest of Islam and the Muslims, then it is permissible — indeed recommended — for the Muslims to contribute to it and take part in accomplishing it.

And there are found in the biography of the Prophet ﷺ many examples of the relationship of partnership and participation with the unbelievers in matters of good, repelling evil and deterring from injustice — the most prominent of them the pact which the Prophet ﷺ contracted

[p. 300] in participation with the unbelievers, by the name "Ḥilf al-Fuḍūl" (the Pact of the Virtuous). And here for you are its details in what follows:

### Ḥilf al-Fuḍūl (the Pact of the Virtuous)

The pacts of mutual cooperation took place in the Jāhiliyya on the basis of the tribe, in the sense that a person was committed to helping whoever belonged to his tribe, whether in the right or the wrong, just or unjust, wrongdoer or wronged. So in all these cases it was a necessity on a person to support his tribesman, even if he was upon clear injustice and evident aggression.

And Ḥilf al-Fuḍūl was the noblest pact ever heard of and the most honourable among the Arabs. And the first to speak of it and call to it was al-Zubayr ibn ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib. And its cause was that a man of Zubayd came to Mecca with goods, and al-ʿĀṣ ibn Wāʾil bought them from him; and he had power and nobility in Mecca, so he withheld his right from him. So the Zubaydī sought help against him from the allies: ʿAbd al-Dār, Makhzūm, Jumaḥ, Sahm and ʿAdī ibn Kaʿb; but they refused to help him against al-ʿĀṣ ibn Wāʾil and rebuked him — that is, they repelled him. When the Zubaydī saw evil, he went up on Abū Qubays at sunrise, while Quraysh were in their gatherings around the Kaʿba, and cried out at the top of his voice:

> *O family of Fihr, for one wronged in his goods,*
> *in the heart of Mecca, far from home and kin…*

And at any rate, in view of the aforementioned hadith in which the Prophet ﷺ praised Ḥilf al-Fuḍūl and showed his resolve to answer [it] if he were called to it today, ʿAllāma al-Suhaylī (may God have mercy on him) and others of the jurists held that it is permissible for the Muslims — indeed recommended — that they enter with non-Muslims into a covenant aiming at helping the wronged and repelling injustice and the like of that of the good purposes beneficial to humanity.

### Jihad

And it has become clear from the detail that has passed that the relationship of the Islamic state with non-Muslims — its manner is defined and its position specified by the conduct of the unbelievers and their way of acting, as God the Exalted said: **"So as long as they are upright toward you, be upright toward them"** [al-Tawba, 9:7].

But the elements which form a real threat to Islam and the Muslims, and which have advanced their horses and their foot-soldiers to show them enmity, and have made them a prey to the arrows of their aggression and persecution of them; and which try to obstruct the course of the Islamic call by putting obstacles in its way; or whose character and occupation corruption and destruction against humanity have become — the Islamic government founded on straight foundations does not know, to deter them from their errors, any option except jihad and struggle, armed and unarmed.

For God, and He is the most truthful of speakers, said: **"They ask you about the sacred month — about fighting therein. And the ruling of jihad for restraining the rein of *fitna* is not confined to what if those persecuted were persecuted among the Muslims; rather whenever the unbelievers set up in the world a tyrannical, oppressive, despotic system, and began to bind God's servants in it with their man-made rulings and their various laws, the Islamic Sharia commands its followers to fight them in the like of this form, to rescue humanity from their injustice by breaking their power and refuting their dominance, and striving to replace the just divine system in its place. And this is what we want to include among the aims which Islam seeks behind the legitimacy of fighting, and it is what is meant in His words, the Exalted: **"And fight them until there is no fitna and [until] the religion, all of it, is for God. ## Chapter Six: On Removing the Imam and Overthrowing the Government

### Introduction

[p. 305] Many Western writers have expressed their opinion that Islam does not permit following any way to topple the government and remove it once it has been formed and established. And perhaps its source is nothing but the superficial, hasty look at the Islamic rulings which emphasize the prohibition of rebellion (*al-khurūj*) against the imam and forbid it categorically. But it is in reality a pure error which has no basis at all from argument or reason. Rather, just as Islam possesses within its folds safe ways for appointing the imam and designating the caliph, likewise its teachings, at the same time, enjoy their comprehensiveness of the manner of removing him and deposing him by calm, peaceful ways also.

### 1 – Peaceful, safe removal

So the following causes are the peaceful matters which make the imam removed from his office from the Sharia side:

**(1)** If he removed himself — that is, he submitted the resignation and showed his inability to continue the work as caliph and imam; for the Sharia judges in this form that he is removed, as is apparent.

**(2)** That something befall him which prevents him from performing the functions of the imam — as if madness overtook him, or illness struck him which prevented him from undertaking even his ordinary affairs and his daily occupations. So it is self-evident that his removal is judged in this form also.

**(3)** If he committed something of the matters of transgression (*fisq*), like drinking wine and taking bribes and imposing non-Sharia taxes on the subjects, and other than that of the matters of transgression, including injustice and tyranny in rule as well. And its ruling is that he deserves removal for his committing these matters; but fighting him and armed rebellion against him are not yet permissible. And the meaning of this: that the Muslims, if it were possible for them to remove him by a safe way, it would be obligatory on them to do that, as al-Ḥāfiẓ Ibn Ḥajar (may God have mercy on him) said: "What the scholars hold concerning the commanders

[p. 306] of tyranny is that if he is able to depose him without strife (*fitna*) and without injustice, it is obligatory; otherwise the obligation is patience."¹

And the meaning of patience here: the prohibition of raising weapons against him and removing him by acts of armed violence; and exerting efforts and attempts by safe, peaceful ways does not negate it — of what it is possible to delegate the reference concerning it to the shūrā council or the court, to verify whether he deserves his removal for what he committed of transgressions or not. So if it was verified with them, his removal would stand.

**(4)** And if there was not found any of these three matters, but his policies and his plans do not appear in agreement with the interests of the state, such that aversion occurs towards him among the people — does he deserve by this his removal or not? I did not find its ruling expressly stated in the words of the people, neither in negation nor in affirmation. And what is preponderant with me, in view of the general rules, is that the shūrā which chose him and put him in charge of the imamate, if it undertook his removal in the like of these circumstances by peaceful constitutional means, there would not be any harm in it. And it is supported by the hadith of Anas ibn Mālik, may God be pleased with him, that the Messenger of God ﷺ cursed three, among them: "A man who led a people [in prayer] while they disliked him."²

And let it be known that there is talk about the chain of this hadith; but the route by which al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī narrated it as mursal is sound, just as it is soundly narrated as mawqūf from ʿAmr ibn al-Ḥārith ibn al-Muṣṭaliq, may God be pleased with him; and al-Tirmidhī (may God have mercy on him) followed his inclusion of this hadith itself with it.

So the hadith indicated that it is not commendable for a person to insist on the imamate of Muslims who dislike him; so if it were possible to replace him with one better than him and more righteous, its accomplishment would be in agreement with the import of this Prophetic hadith, upon its owner be blessing and peace.

And also, ʿAwf ibn Mālik narrated from the Messenger of God ﷺ; he said: "The best of your imams are those whom you love and who love you, and who pray for you and you pray for them; and the worst of your imams are those whom you hate and who hate you, and whom you curse and who curse you."³

### Rebellion against the imam [undertaking acts of armed violence against the imam]

Lo, do not return after me as misguided ones [or: unbelievers], some of you striking the necks of others. Lo, let the present inform the absent; for perhaps some of those whom it reaches will be more retentive of it than some of those who heard it." Then he said: "Lo, have I conveyed?" twice.¹

And from this we see the Islamic Sharia has sometimes borne heinous wrongs merely for protecting the Muslims from plunging into civil wars. And there passed before us the hadith of ʿAwf ibn Mālik al-Ashjaʿī in which came: "Lo, whoever has a governor appointed over him, and he sees him do something of disobedience to God, let him dislike what he does of disobedience to God, and let him not withdraw a hand from obedience."²

**The second:** the jurists have agreed altogether that rebellion, in all forms of its permissibility, requires two matters: ability and invulnerability (*al-qudra wa-l-manʿa*), and that there not occur from it a harm greater than the harm of this imam's remaining. And this is just as Imam Abū Ḥanīfa (may God have mercy on him), even if he saw rebellion as permissible in itself, nevertheless did not take part in it, giving as reason: "If one man rose for it, he would be killed, and the people's affair would not be set right. But if he found righteous helpers against it, and a man to lead them who is trusted in the religion of God… And this is an obligation which is not like the obligations which a man undertakes alone; and this, whenever the man undertook it alone, he would shed his blood and expose himself to being killed; so I fear that he would help in killing himself."¹

And at any rate, Imam Abū Ḥanīfa and al-Ḥasan ibn Ṣāliḥ ibn Ḥayy (may God have mercy on them both) used to permit armed rebellion against the transgressing imams who administer the helm of government with what entails contempt of the Sharia; but the jurists after them — including the followers of Ḥanafī jurisprudence also — agreed, in the light of their seasoned experiences in this field, that acts of armed violence like this usually do not result, in the right of the Umma, in a better and more ideal outcome; so it is better that one be careful of it and avoid it — as al-Ḥāfiẓ Ibn Ḥajar (may God have mercy on him) said in the biography of al-Ḥasan ibn Ṣāliḥ (may God have mercy on him):

"[Al-Ḥasan ibn Ṣāliḥ] used to see the sword — meaning rebellion against the imams of tyranny; and this is an old school of the predecessors; but the matter settled on abandoning that, because they saw that it led to what is more severe than it. For in the event of al-Ḥarra and the event of Ibn al-Ashʿath and others than them there is an admonition for whoever reflects… And al-Ḥasan, with that, did not rebel against anyone."²

And al-Ḥāfiẓ (may God have mercy on him) himself said, in the explanation of one of the hadiths narrated from Ibn ʿAbbās,

### Political movements

And there is another issue worth mentioning and studying in this discussion of ours, namely: in the form where rebellion against the imam is not permissible, is there then a safe way which Islam permits to dissolve a government and replace others in its place, and to respond to the demands of the people and rescue rights and obtain them in full? How does Islam look at the political movements which are adopted in contemporary political circles to achieve this purpose?

It is a real phenomenon that Western ideas and theories have become — for centuries — the first bricks upon which we Muslims found the structure of our intellectual life and the edifice

> ¹ *Fatḥ al-Bārī* of Ibn Ḥajar (13/7).
> ² *Sharḥ al-Siyar al-Kabīr* (1/869), chapter on hiring in the land of war and the extra share [*nafl*], paragraph no. 1567.

[p. 314] of our civilizational existence in all branches of life in general, and politics among them in particular. So from this many of the political works that were imported from the West have been taken as something like what is necessary for practising political life, regardless of their soundness and their corruption. And among those works: the methods of popular protest prevailing widely, like strikes and demonstrations accompanied by sabotage and destruction, and other than that of the ways people tread to pressure the government and carry it to yield and respond to their demands.

And concerning the ruling of these political works from a Sharia perspective, we say: some of them are definitively forbidden, not permissible in the Sharia at all and absolutely — like abstaining from food to the limit of committing suicide by hunger; and likewise any other method that leads to violating the sanctity of someone's blood or property or honour, or harming public governmental property, or damaging the funds of the state. And aggression against the properties of the state and its funds is a disobedience which is among the greatest disobediences, whose rectification and the release from whose punishment is difficult in every difficulty; by virtue of its not being a special personal property of the rulers only — rather in it is a right for all the citizens of the state and its inhabitants. So toying with it and inflicting any harm on it means injuring the right of the whole people. And since it relates to the rights of servants, release from it is not possible except by being absolved by all the holders of rights — and how is a person to be absolved, or to seek release, from all the inhabitants of the state?! So his shoulder then remains weighed down with the burden of this great, enormous disobedience.

As for the ruling of the general strike, we say: if one of the people appealed to them and asked them to express their anger at a governmental measure and their resentment at it by suspending their commercial works for a period, then left them free to respond to this request or not — there is not, in the mere request or responding to it, any harm in the Sharia, if the people disliked it and were not compelled. For a strike like this is permissible in itself in the Sharia as a permissible measure for attaining the permissible goal — on condition that there be excepted in it what is indispensable for human beings, like medical care and others, and especially in the state of emergencies.

But it has become an inseparable part of the strike that whoever appeals for it and calls to it does not content himself

[p. 315] with appealing and calling; rather the situation has deteriorated to the point that they compel the people to contribute to their strike and force them to suspend their works and close their commercial shops, and rain stones on whoever dared to take his car out into the street, and block the streets and public roads, and use violence against whoever does not contribute to the strike — or at least pour upon them the wrath of their anger and stir up against them the revolt of their resentment. And it is not hidden that all these works are definitively forbidden in the Sharia; besides that they cause several other evils as well: for the poor worker who earns his daily food is deprived, he and his family, of their provision; and the sick one who suffers from the severity of his illness — loss of treatment increases him in suffering and hardship; and some of them meet their death because of their inability to obtain treatment.

And what one never ceases to be astonished and amazed at in this regard is: the double standard of democracy and its treatment with two faces: for it claims to support freedom of expression and opinion on one side, while it does not acknowledge, on another side, for whoever does not contribute to the strike his right to anything in these freedoms, by its resorting to compelling contribution in it whether he wills or refuses. So this is among what clashes with the principles of Islam and contradicts moral values and principles, and specifically the principle of respecting freedom of opinion and thought.

And rarely is a strike free of these evils and harms in this age of ours; and that a strike of a safe, peaceful kind should occur in this atmosphere of ours, whose appealers have sat with manliness and nobility, leaving to the people the way to respond to their calls or not — that is of rarity and strangeness in a place not near.

And the permissible act, if it is taken as a means to forbidden things and a way to committing what is not permissible in the Sharia, then it should be forbidden — that permissible thing — in keeping with the principle of blocking the means (*sadd al-dharāʾiʿ*). And from this, the measure of the strike which produces destruction and sabotage, and the disturbance of security and peace, and obstructing people from continuing their works, does not fall within the measures permitted in the Sharia.

And since it is established that politics is not intended in itself — rather the original intent and the ultimate goal is attaining the pleasure of God, glorified and exalted — therefore let us take the ways and methods which our Lord, majestic and exalted, is pleased with, of what does not contradict the Sharia; otherwise there is no meaning to attempting

[p. 316] to implement Islam by way of contradicting its rulings one after the other.

And marches and demonstrations — their ruling likewise is that they are permissible in themselves if they do not cause severe harm to the people; but their nature, like the strike, rarely is free also of acts of violence, sabotage and causing problems for the people; and it is apparent that the saying of their permissibility from this aspect is not possible.

And after this detail, the question which we put forward at the beginning of the discussion repeats itself here: is there a way other than them for pressuring the government and obtaining rights from it?

So in answer to it we say: the truth is that the Sharia has guided us to a way and taught us a principle — if we took it with regard, the tyrannical governments would surrender to it and the despotic authorities would submit to it — namely what the Prophet ﷺ expressed by his saying: "No obedience to a creature in disobedience to the Creator."

So if the people received it once and put it always before its eyes, so it refused altogether to act by what contradicts the Sharia of the commands of the government and its rulings — then by God tell me: what then remains with the government of a trick, and in its quiver of an arrow for management?! And suppose that all the judges working in the courts announced their refusal to work as long as it is not permitted for them to rule by the rulings of the Sharia; and the lawyers, all of them, if they determined their resolve on not appearing before the courts in the capacity of advocacy as long as its laws are not dyed with a religious dye; and the employees of the banks, if they all refrained from providing their services in them, and the beneficiaries of them — the depositors — abstained, to the last of them, from depositing funds in them; and the merchants, all of them, from financing facilities as long as their system is not purified of usury — if all of these undertook this strike built on the basis of the aforementioned Sharia rule [no obedience to a creature in disobedience to the Creator], they would find it more useful for their purpose and more damaging to the governmental bodies — a damage that would make them humble, subject, surrendering in the hands of their demands and their needs.

So this is the Sharia method for showing protest against the government; except that — since it needs something of sacrifice — people do not find it palatable and the people do not find it easy. For it is very easy for a person to repeat the shouts for the life of Islam and raise slogans in its right while he practises

[p. 317] with them usurious transactions with all freedom; and likewise it is easy — how easy it is! — that the judge and the lawyer be occupied with undertaking their professions according to man-made laws even if clashing with the rulings of the Sharia, and with that they contribute to the demonstrations in the right of Islam and raise their throats with the tunes of its life. As for using these professions and functions of theirs which they practise — of the judiciary, advocacy, banking and others — as a weapon to attain the goal of implementing Islam, it needs something of resolve and sacrifice; and how much we need them and how much they are lacking among us!

We grant that this way only avails and returns with fruit if the majority of the people is prepared for it, or at least a large number of them for whom the government reckons and gives weight, such that it suffices for pressuring it. And it is known that preparing this amount is not completed except after directing the people and making it aware — what needs an organized planning on the part of the parties and political groups if they first opened their minds so that this idea becomes rooted in them, and made their breasts welcome to accept this principle; and then that they undertake preparing a programme and planning a project aiming at the awareness of the people and its education in this field, beginning from the educational institutions and ending with the public mass meetings. So if this campaign undertook acquainting people with the harms of the jobs in which they are compelled to cooperate in forbidden matters, and their grasping the extent of the value of the lawful and its gravity — so that it made the majority of the people averse to them and dislike them, and then prepared to abandon them even at the cost of material sacrifice and of living a less comfortable and luxurious life than it was before — then this campaign would obtain a dazzling success and an overwhelming victory, and would pave the way for implementing the Sharia more than any strike or demonstration.

And this, just as the army has often undertaken suspending the constitution or cancelling it in Pakistan and other states, and succeeded in that — because every time there was found among the judges of the higher courts someone who granted it a Sharia document for its action. But some of them refused the agreement to any measure contrary to the constitution; so they — even if they lost their jobs and were deprived of material facilities — at the same time obtained from the people trust, appreciation and respect for their action and praise of their role, to the point that they saw others besides them of the judges imitate them; and they became certain that it would not be within the capacity of

[p. 318] anyone to cancel the constitution if they all refused to document it legitimately. And this actually happened when the great number of the judges of the Supreme Court in Pakistan agreed on opposing what General Pervez Musharraf undertook of cancelling the constitution; so the world saw, and witnessed with its own eyes, that success in the end was not except for those judges, not for the tyrannical despot.

And as we presented in what has passed, the basic constitution of the Islamic state and its primary sources of legislation are nothing but the Qur'an and the Prophetic Sunna — and the constitution of Pakistan has provided for that, praise be to God. So if the motives for preserving them were aroused in the people, and the sentiments of protecting them were stirred in it as they were against Pervez Musharraf, this principle [no obedience to a creature in disobedience to the Creator] would play its role greatly, as being a method of splendour and of great effect for safe, peaceful protest.

### Political movements and the aspect of self-reform

And this point too is of gravity and standing in the subject: that Islam does not aspire, from political efforts and movements, to obtaining authority or possessing power over lands and servants; rather the ultimate goal of them in Islam is: reforming the system of government, seeking the pleasure of God, glorified and exalted, by making it in agreement with His Sharia, and administering it according to what He loves and is pleased with. And since it is not necessary for achieving this goal that the holders of these efforts and movements themselves reach the seats of authority, there is therefore no need, from the point of view of Sharia consideration, for ascending the throne of authority if it were possible to attain it by way of reforming the people of the seats who are established upon them, or by way of others than them — groups or individuals.

And the first thing stipulated for this is that these workers in the field of Islamic politics belonging to its groups be sincere of intention, sound of disposition, free of the taints of love of status and position and seeking command and authority.
