
[p. 63] **"…They did not kill him, nor did they crucify him, but it was made to appear so to them. Those who differ over it are in doubt about it; they have no knowledge of it except the following of conjecture. They did not kill him, for certain. Rather, God raised him to Himself…"** [al-Nisāʾ, 4:157–158].

After his raising to heaven, his followers remained overpowered and defeated in the world for three centuries. Christian historians call this age "the Age of Persecution", and truly in that age a severe ordeal and calamity befell them: many kings raided them, scattered them and destroyed them, among them the famous king Nero, of whom the saying became proverbial: "While Rome was burning, Nero was playing the flute."

After three hundred years a fortunate day came upon Christianity, when the Roman emperor Constantine embraced it and adopted it as the official religion of the state, which gave it strength and lasting control. This was the beginning of the Christian kingdom, which lasted over Rome for a long period, in which the king was subject to the Church. Finally this Christian kingdom was divided into two parts: the western part, whose capital was Rome — the present-day capital of Italy — and the eastern part, whose capital was Constantinople, today called Istanbul. Following the division of the kingdom, the Church too was divided into two factions: a western one, with its seat in Rome, and an eastern one, with its seat in Constantinople; and their names also changed and differed. The Roman one was called "the Roman Catholic Church" and its head "the Pope", while the eastern one that split from it was called "the Holy Orthodox Church", and its head: the Patriarch. The word "Orthodox" means: firmly rooted in creed; it claimed that it was the one firm and rooted in the Christian creeds. Even when Constantinople was conquered at the hands of Sultan Mehmed, the head of its church used to call himself "the Patriarch of Great Constantinople". Behind this division lay several prominent factors, both political, religious and racial. At any rate, the conflicts remained raging and wars broke out between these two churches for a long period — what Christian historians call "the Great Schism".

[p. 64] Then came a period in which this schism multiplied among them, until from each of the two kingdoms other kingdoms split off, and from each of the two churches other churches; the number of popes reached four after there had been only two, and a series of civil wars began that stretched without end. In that period there occurred the incidents of corruption, arbitrariness and aggression that we explained earlier: trading in indulgences, criticism of the Pope's commands became an unforgivable crime, translation of the Holy Bible was categorically forbidden, and the dissenters who departed from the system were burned in the street openly — among them John Huss and "Jerome". However, the movement of renaissance and awakening stirred up by John Huss did not subside or calm down despite the injustice, aggression and repression, until "Martin Luther" was born and founded the Protestant sect, with which he managed to eliminate what remained of the spirit of the papacy.

"Protestant" means "the protesters", being derived from "protest". This name is applied to it because it arose on protest and objection against the Roman Catholic Church and its papacy. Martin rose up and said to them: "You declare a heretic anyone who raises his voice against you, and punish him with severe, harsh punishments; yet in truth you are more deserving of this name, since you have introduced into Christianity beliefs and rites that have no connection with it at all. We believe only in the pure, unadulterated beliefs mentioned in the Holy Bible; as for what you have invented, we reject it outright as worthless!"

There are differences distinguishing the Protestant denomination from the followers of the Roman Catholic Church, the most important of which is that it denies "the Lord's Supper", which forms an important part of the beliefs of the Roman Church. Its origin among the Christians is that Jesus, upon him be peace, ate before the alleged arrest one day with his disciples and followers a meal including bread and some broth, and said after that to his disciples: "Do this in remembrance of me." The Roman Catholic Church interpreted this saying to mean that it became obligatory on every Christian to perform a special rite of that meal. The manner of celebrating this rite is that the Pope takes bread and touches it to a part of his body, and so [p. 65] that bread is transformed, according to their belief, into the body of the Messiah, and that broth into his blood. The aim of performing this rite is to show faith in the doctrine of atonement. So Luther rose up, denied this rite and rejected faith in this doctrine.

The second difference is that the Protestant denomination does not recognize some books of the Holy Bible. In detail: the Holy Bible among the Christians is the term for the book that brings together all the books and scriptures revealed to Moses and Jesus, upon them both be peace, and to their disciples and others after them. The first five books (Genesis, Exodus, Leviticus, Numbers, Deuteronomy) are called "the Torah". Then scriptures continued to be revealed to the prophets of the Children of Israel, upon them be peace, among them: the Psalms of David and the Proverbs of Solomon, upon them both be peace, and the rest of the thirty-eight books revealed before Jesus, upon him be peace; their collection is called "the Old Testament". After that there are four Gospels about the life of Jesus, upon him be peace — the Gospel of Matthew, the Gospel of Mark, the Gospel of Luke and the Gospel of John — followed by the Acts of the Apostles and the Epistles; their collection is called the New Testament. The collection of these books in both Testaments is called the Holy Bible. There are thirteen books which the Roman Church believes in and which the Protestants do not recognize; rather, they describe them as the Apocrypha, i.e. the spurious books or those whose authenticity is doubted. Hence the Holy Bible differs for the two denominations, since the Romans include in their Holy Bible these thirteen books, while the Holy Bible among the Protestants is devoid of them.

The third difference between them is that the Protestant denomination does not grant the Pope absolute authority and despotic rule, nor does it make the authority to interpret the Holy Bible exclusive to the Pope; rather, it entrusts it to everyone who has acquired knowledge. Nor do they have those ranks for priests and men of religion that existed among the Romans.

Finally, the king of England, "Henry" the Eighth, was influenced by this movement, so he supported and aided it, until by virtue of his support it became a powerful adversary to the Catholic Church, just as Christianity had grown strong in [p. 66] its early days through the Roman emperor Constantine's embrace of it. Although Catholicism still represents the majority of Christians to this day, the Christians who live in our lands of the Indian subcontinent usually belong to Protestantism.

Thus intellectual freedom began among the Christians. The second reason for that goes back to the Crusades that broke out between the Christians and the Muslims under the leadership of Nūr al-Dīn Zangī and Ṣalāḥ al-Dīn al-Ayyūbī and others, in the wake of which Jerusalem was conquered and the Muslims were able to recover it after the Christians had held it for eighty years. The mingling of Christians with Muslims thus increased on the one hand; and on the other, the catastrophe of the fall of al-Andalus resulted in the transfer of the sciences of the Muslims to Europe and the opportunity for it to benefit from them. Individuals among them took up the continuation of the work of investigation and research following the methods the Muslims had pursued and the paths they had trodden, which was the beginning of Europe's development and the opening of its flourishing in these sciences. That era is called "the New Renaissance". For they regarded the ages before it as "Dark Ages", since the Church had imposed strict restrictions on freedom of thought and expression; those ages, for the new Western world, were the equivalent of the ages of the Jāhiliyya before the dawn of Islam. However, they regarded them as dark ages for the whole of humanity, even though they were the ages in which the banner of Islam extended across the inhabited world, near and far, and in which the Muslims reached the zenith of flourishing and the summit of development in the sciences and the height of splendour in civilization, while Europe floundered in the darkness of ignorance and stumbled in the depths of gloom. So it would have been right for it to regard them — with respect to itself specifically — as the dark ages.

They were followed by the age of "Rationalism", when the champions of the renaissance and the advocates of revival cried out that every claim of religion must be put to the touchstone of reason, and that we should cast aside everything our minds do not find acceptable; and they began to launch raids on religious beliefs on the basis of reason. Then the philosophy arose that emphasizes freedom and equality and regards every person as absolutely free in his thought, belief and conduct; it is called liberalism. When their minds were freed, [p. 67] they went in this liberation in every direction, crossed every boundary and exceeded every measure, to the point that there appeared in their midst seeds of doubt and suspicion about the existence of God the Exalted (and refuge is sought with God), and then open denial of the existence of God became general!

This, then, was a simple, brief picture of the history of Christianity and Europe!

#### The feudal system

We were in the course of explaining the system of aristocracy, among the political systems the world has witnessed in some of its periods, and I had explained its three types: intellectual, racial and religious. In the course of discussing religious aristocracy — and specifically Christian — we digressed into a brief account of the history of Christianity and Europe, because of its role in understanding the political systems arising from it. Now we want to turn our attention to explaining the feudal system as a fourth type of aristocracy. I have seen fit to include it among its types — even though scholars of political science do not list it as one of its types — first for the sake of introducing it, and second because it falls within its broad concept mentioned at the beginning of the discussion: that aristocracy also covers the case in which a class acquires great influence and far-reaching interference in government circles, such that it becomes in fact and reality the ruling party. That too is regarded as aristocracy, even if that particular class does not exercise rule directly. Moreover, I would like to acquaint you with a mass of political notions scattered in the corners of books, and I could not pass over so important a system as feudalism with my eyes closed.

The feudal system is called in English "Feudalism". There is another aspect that makes us focus on dwelling at length on defining it, namely that its standing is similar to that of theocracy, in that people — and especially those among them enlightened by Western culture — have often tried to distance themselves from it with the justification that it brings to the Umma nothing but evils and woes, by analogy with what they have heard about the distorted Christian theocracy, which is closer to the papacy than to divine government. Likewise they have distorted the reputation of the feudal system and tried to exclude it by presenting the distorted picture that prevailed in [p. 68] Europe; and exaggeration in rejecting it may have led them to condemning the system of land grants (*iqṭāʿ*) that is legitimate in Islam — especially in a period when the star of socialism and communism was rising, when they accused the scholars of supporting, strengthening and championing the feudal system. Hence, in view of these factors, it is necessary to go deeply into studying and understanding the feudal system that prevailed in Europe in times past.

Its story in fact goes back to the centuries of medieval Europe, which passed through centuries in which the mill of war turned, clashes broke out, and conflicts raged: either civil wars between its peoples — like France and England, for example — or with the Muslims, as Islam had begun to grow and rise as a force of great might. As a result of these successive wars, a severe economic crisis befell it, such that it was no longer able to bear those exorbitant sums that the wars required, which doubled day by day alongside development in the arts of war and its machines and equipment — not to mention the high cost that the commanders of armies and tribal chiefs on the battlefields received, such as the horsemen known as Knights, each of whom was a daring, brave and bold hero, and whom Europe used to employ as mercenaries for raiding the Muslims in the Crusades. Their salaries and demands, in addition to the machines and military equipment of horses and weapons, cost Europe exorbitant sums. So, out of the necessity of resisting this reality and the need to face that situation, a system was brought into being that was later called the feudal system.

As has been customary with humanity — that it first implements a system out of the motive of necessity and the drive of need, then invents a philosophy or idea supporting that system and conferring legitimacy on it — here too it invented a philosophy on the basis of land tenure, namely: that land is what forms a bond linking ruler and ruled and connects them to one another. The one who holds the land is the ruling master, and the one who rents it and uses it in agriculture is the ruled peasant. Since all land belongs to God, He is the first master; and since He enabled the king to dispose of it, the king is ruled by Him and subordinate. When the king granted it to the fief-holders (*muqṭaʿīn*), he became their master and the fief-holders subject to him; then when the fief-holders delegated it to the peasants, they became their heads and the peasants their subordinates. [p. 69] Likewise the peasants among themselves: the greater of them is master of the lesser, and the holder of much is head over the holder of little. Government, rule and being ruled — all these meanings were tied to their connection with land. The king is responsible before the Lord, glorified and exalted, since He granted him the land, with complete power to dispose of it as he wished; so when he gave someone a piece of it, that man to whom it was given became subject to the king in that piece of land, in return for complete power to dispose of it as he wished and willed. Thus an endless, unbroken chain of rule and being ruled was formed on the basis of plots of land. Let us give an example: the king granted Zayd ten thousand hectares of land, for example, so Zayd is regarded as master and ruler of that piece of land, with the choice of disposing of it as he wishes. He then divided it into parts and plots, then gave it to other people such as ʿAmr, Bakr and Khālid. All of these who received land from him are regarded as subordinates of Zayd within the framework of the land they obtained. Then ʿAmr, Bakr and Khālid granted the lands they had received from Zayd to other fief-holders, and they are regarded as the masters and heads over them; then these divided it among the peasants, and so on. The land was not granted to anyone for free, but in return for conditions, obligations and services that had to be fulfilled. The king, when granting it to someone, would stipulate that he undertake to give him such-and-such an amount of financial returns as land tax (*kharāj*), and to provide him from his subjects with such-and-such a number of soldiers and weapons if the king faced a state of war. Thus the relationship between the king and his subjects was confined and restricted to his giving them land and their paying him the land tax and providing military services in war.

The feudal lord was the absolute ruler and despotic master over all the inhabitants of the plot of land that the king had granted him. He imposed crushing taxes on them, extracted exorbitant rents from them, imposed harsh, crippling conditions on them, and disposed of them as a despotic king disposes of his subjects — indeed as a master disposes of his slaves and servants! In return for the land-lease contract and the undertaking to protect them — protection being incumbent on the fief-holding lord — and to defend them, and to try to obtain privileges and exemptions for them from the king. Then the lesser one dealt with those beneath him likewise, [p. 70] and this chain continued down to the lowest individual among the subjects.

Everyone who owned land and gave it to someone else was called "the feudal lord" or "the grantor" (*al-muqṭiʿ*), and the one directly beneath him was called "the grantee" (*al-muqṭaʿ*), i.e. the vassal, and the land he gave was called "the fief" (*al-iqṭāʿa*).

Among the duties of the ruled subordinate was to provide military services to the chief master, each to the one above him, until it reached the king; and they might take turns in these services by distributing them across the days of the year, some performing them in one period and others in another. Thus they tried to eliminate the problems of wars and cover their costs.

Second, it was stipulated on them to pay the land tax of his lands or their rents to the master (the lord).

Third, the lord would keep a plot of land for himself to be farmed directly; the tenant farmers had to work on his land in farming, and they followed in this too the method of rotation, some people working in it in one period of the year and others in another.

Fourth, when the lord's son grew up and reached maturity, the tenants had to contribute their share to raising him to the rank of knights, bearing his costs and covering his expenses.

Fifth, they had to provide their financial services whenever the lord wished to marry off his daughter.

Sixth, if the lord fell captive in the hands of an enemy, the tenants were obliged to free him and rescue him by whatever means were possible for them: if by ransom, they had to spend money on it; and if by persons, they had to provide persons to become hostages of the captors in place of the lord. (As when the enemy demanded ten persons in return for releasing the lord: the subjects had to provide them to him so that the lord could be freed.)

Seventh, whenever the lord went on a journey, an outing or hunting, the subjects had to provide him with support and comfort in it, host him, and prepare for him the best and most comfortable place for hunting.

The lord was the one who inherited from the farmer if he died without direct heirs (*kalāla*); and if the farmer [p. 71] opposed one of his rulings or disobeyed one of his commands, the lord had the right to confiscate his land and take it back from him. All of this was among the covenants concluded between the lord and the tenants.

This was a brief review of the harsh, crippling conditions imposed on the tenants by the feudal lords.

Then, when the feudal lords acquired full authority and complete power over the farmers and tenants beneath them, they occupied the positions of government and took up the reins of authority in that region, until their authority gradually turned into a government equivalent to and alongside the king's government. Thus they acquired control over the whole state. The king gave them free rein to lay down whatever legislation they wished and enact whatever laws they wished, and whatever conditions they found palatable to themselves over their subjects — some of their conditions mentioned in books have been stated above, expressly and explicitly. Moreover, it is not difficult to appreciate the extent of the severity of the conditions imposed on them in view of the severe harshness and complete power acquired by the feudal lords as a result of the king's submission to them in return for their helping and supporting him in wars. They were in fact the ones who controlled the system in reality, such that they remained able to force the king to implement whatever laws they wished and issue whatever commands they wanted. The alliance of the two factions — the kings and the feudal lords — enabled this system to run on its wheels for a time; then it was followed by bloody wars and ferocious massacres in the wake of the clash of interests and conflict between the two sides. History has preserved for us a great deal of the incidents of their conflict.

This is a simple picture of the feudal system that prevailed in Europe in the Middle Ages. It is not hidden from any intelligent person what massive evils and widespread abuses accompanied it, beginning with the enslavement by the feudal lords of the farmers beneath them and their oppression of them, and ending with their rebellion against the king's system and their actual control over the policy of the state and its affairs, such that it was impossible for any matter to be concluded or any decision to be taken in it without the agreement and approval of these people. It remained in force in Europe for long years, and it groaned under its evils and woes throughout that period.

[p. 72] It was natural that it should become an insult and a disgrace, so that the word does not strike the ear today without the mind rushing to that ugly system and its hideous picture.

Several manifestations of it emerged in the Islamic world, especially in some eras of the Ottoman Caliphate and the Mughal state in India. But nevertheless it was not an exact copy of that unjust European feudal system, nor was it accompanied by that harsh severity that leads to enslaving the farmers and breaking their backs. It was merely similar to it in the stipulation that the feudal lords provide military services with machines and personnel. Among them was one titled "feudal lord of five thousand", meaning that he must provide five thousand soldiers to the king if he needed them in wars; and among them was one titled "feudal lord of ten thousand", and so on. As for the English, by virtue of their being accustomed to that feudal system and fond of it, when they took control of India and assumed the reins of its government, they implemented it in several of its regions almost exactly on the original pattern. Let us give an example of that with the system of chieftaincy in Baluchistan: the authority of the chiefs there in reality goes back to their having received fiefs from the English, so they treated their subjects with the same harshness and severity with which the European feudal lords treated the farmers beneath them.

Now, perhaps you have noticed — in the light of the aforementioned details — the vast gulf and clear difference between the feudal system that prevailed in Europe and what Islam has legislated by way of land grants (*iqṭāʿ*). For the iqṭāʿ legitimate in Islam — by which we mean the sultan's giving a man land and assigning it to him — has no connection whatsoever with European feudalism. Its legitimacy rests on the action of the Prophet ﷺ himself, who granted land to each of Wāʾil ibn Ḥujr, Muʿāwiya, ʿUmar al-Fārūq and Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq, may God be pleased with them all, and to many other people.

The difference between the two systems lies, first, in the motivating purpose of its procedures and legitimacy. European feudalism was implemented for the purpose of covering the costs of wars and providing military services, while Islamic iqṭāʿ was for the benefit of the state and its welfare in terms of its role in reviving dead, barren lands and reclaiming them for agriculture. Hence it was stipulated for [p. 73] the grantees that they undertake to cultivate it within three years; if they did so [well and good], otherwise it was taken back from them because the intended aim had not been fulfilled. It might have been given to needy poor people in order to support them financially and try to raise their economic standard. Thus Islam did not aim, behind granting lands, except that the grantees should revive and farm them — themselves or through their workers — and consequently play their role in increasing the state's produce and its output. These lands entered their ownership after reviving and farming them, and they had the choice of handing them to the peasants for sharecropping (*muzāraʿa*), observing the conditions the Sharia established for it, without departing from it by a hand-span. They could not stipulate on the farmers anything the Sharia does not permit by way of corrupt conditions, let alone harsh, crippling conditions! Among the most prominent of those conditions, and the most important, is that the transaction must not be on the basis of a specific amount from the produce for one of the two parties; rather it must be on the basis of sharing — halves or thirds and so on. Sharecropping is thus no more than a simple contract concluded by the two parties like other contracts such as sale and lease, and it does not make the owner of the land a ruler and chief, nor the farmer a follower and subordinate at all! So much so that he has no right to stipulate on the farmer whatever conditions he wishes and gain through them whatever benefits he wishes, such as financial support in arming the son or marrying the daughter, or that he be the sole heir after his death, or that he demand from him a specific, set amount of money. The upshot is that Islamic iqṭāʿ has no connection, however slight, with European feudalism!

If this is established, we can arrive with all clarity and explicitness at the conclusion that what these modernists utter whenever they hear the word iqṭāʿ — accusing the scholars of supporting and implementing the European feudal system — is chatter that has no share of sound evidence, upright reasoning or reasonable argument!

Second, the other matter distinguishing the two kinds of iqṭāʿ is that the obligations the Sharia imposed on the owner of land lie in one of two matters: the tithe (*ʿushr*) or the land tax (*kharāj*). Both are known and specified in all their forms and details, and neither the grantee, the landowner nor anyone else has any scope to change [p. 74] or dispose of them. Hence, if Islamic iqṭāʿ were applied, there would be no dangers or fears of being accompanied by those abuses that were inseparable parts of European feudalism. This point should be emphasized and given attention because we have seen people use it to mislead others and distort their belief about Islamic iqṭāʿ, by explaining the abuses of the European system first, and then applying it to Islamic iqṭāʿ second.

#### Fascism

The fifth type of aristocracy is: fascist aristocracy. It is a political system brought into being by the Italian dictator Mussolini. The word is derived from the Greek [sic: Italian *fascio*, from Latin *fasces*] word "fascio", which is taken from the Arabic word *al-faʾs* (the axe), an iron tool used for digging, cutting, splitting wood and the like. It is also applied to a bundle of rods from which the handle of an axe is made. The Italian dictator Mussolini had taken the axe as a traditional symbol of his government, like the sickle in Russia, and like the crescent in the middle of which is a star on the flag of Pakistan. So the axe adorned the official flag of the government and all its other emblems. From this, the precise rendering of the word "fascism" is: axe-monarchy.

The background of this system is that the First World War had brought Italy to a deteriorating economic situation, despite its siding with the Allies who won the war. The state was on the verge of collapse, and disturbances and pervasive anarchy prevailed in it. Market activity was paralysed, government departments failed, the state lost its prestige, and successive governments were unable to resolve the crisis that had taken hold of it. In those dire circumstances and deteriorating conditions Mussolini began his journey by founding his fascist party, and quickly gained popular support for the promises he made that he would revive the economy, bring prosperity and restore the glory of Italy that it had enjoyed in the era of the Roman Empire. In 1922 the fascists grew strong, and Mussolini marched with his followers on Rome in black shirts, and forced the king of Italy to appoint Mussolini as prime minister, threatening him with seizing and taking over [p. 75] power if he did not. The king submitted to their demands and called on Mussolini to form the government. No sooner had Mussolini ascended the seat of power than he began laying the foundations of dictatorship, and banned all political parties except the fascist party.

The gist of Mussolini's fascism is that the state — which represents the nation (*al-qawm*) — is above all, and the individual is nothing. This is the same as what Iqbal said in his well-known Urdu verse:

> *Fard qāʾim rabṭ-e millat se hai, tanhā kuchh nahīn*
> *Mawj hai daryā men aur bērūn-e daryā kuchh nahīn*

[That is:] The individual is sustained by his bond with his community (*milla*) and nation, while in himself he is nothing. It is like the wave that flows with all its force rushing forward as long as it is in the river; but outside the river it has no force to make it flow — rather it quickly dissipates.

Thus Mussolini presented the idea that the individual has no standing in himself, so he does not refrain from sacrificing him for the sake of the collective. His philosophy focused on reviving the glory of the nation and raising it to the stages of perfection, even if that compelled him to invade other countries and occupy them for the benefit of his nation. It made it obligatory for the government to interfere in — indeed to dominate completely — all activities: education, upbringing, law, economics and so on. This requires it to be strong and firmly rooted, not bound to follow the masses; rather, it must follow every path and pursue every method it deems more fitting and more appropriate for the benefit of the collective and the development of the state, without fear holding it back from undertaking it or a deterrent restraining it from adopting it. This happens only if the whole people is subject and subordinate to the state, without having any right to oppose it or disagree with it in anything.

There is no doubt that Mussolini played a prominent role in developing his nation and raising up his state, and resolved to a great extent the economic crisis that faced Italy in the wake of the First World War, just as he succeeded in reviving the national and nationalist (*qawmiyya*) spirit. He used to say: we do not believe in the principle of non-violence, for it is a path — or say: a way out — for cowards. So we will not refrain from plunging into [p. 76] the battlefield to glorify our nation and establish its prestige over other nationalities, and the wars will bring our community (*milla*) the glad tidings of new life.

His saying became proverbial: "War is for men as childbirth is for women." Just as a woman cannot accomplish a creative work or the creation of generations and peoples unless she goes through the process of childbirth, so men cannot achieve a splendid creative work except by plunging into conquest and wars; and wars are what will bring him dominance and sovereignty and restore to him confidence in his nation and his homeland. So he should never refrain from plunging into the field of fighting!

He used to say: we do not believe in democracy at all; for it requires a person to follow the opinion of the majority, whereas it is not necessarily the case that the majority's opinion is always and absolutely right. The majority may agree on a matter that is in reality more dangerous and more harmful to the interests of the state and the benefit of the nation. Hence the fascist philosophy concentrates all authority in the class of leaders of the fascist party, and subjects the whole people to its rule and opinion.

Third, it does not believe in the principle of personal freedom, since the individual is not respected in himself, but in view of his being an individual of his nation and a part of his homeland. So if the national interests required the enactment of a law or the issuing of rulings imposing a limit on his personal freedom, that is the very essence of right and entirely in accord with wisdom! Indeed, the government must adopt it, whatever its effect on the personal freedom of the individual and his self-interest!

Accordingly, the fascist party did not allow any other party to arise or to oppose its policy. The only way to criticize the system is for a person to join the fascist party and become a member of it, then inform it of the reform he wants in the system! As for confronting the government and its policies with criticism through a press statement or a popular meeting, that was never permitted. Thus personal freedom was completely taken away, and the philosophy of the sovereignty of the collective and the domination of the group prevailed.

Since governments founded on the basis of fascism completely dominate all [p. 77] the activities of life and the branches of humanity, they were called "totalitarian governments", meaning that no branch of the branches of life continues its activities except under the control of the apparatus of government. When broad powers were delegated to the government and personal freedom was taken away, it was natural that injustice and aggression should result from them — and this is what actually happened in fascism!

#### Nazi government

After Mussolini, Hitler adopted fascism, and his fascism was an exact copy of Mussolini's fascism, except that he added racial nationalism (*al-qawmiyya al-ʿunṣuriyya*) to it. His philosophy focused on the idea that rule and sovereignty are deserved only by those who belong to the Aryan [race]; for he saw them as a superior people, while other racial minorities were of a lower degree. Proceeding from this belief, he became a fierce enemy to the Semitic races, and made their extermination a goal pursued throughout his life, and resolved to continue invading and fighting them until he succeeded in destroying them, suppressing them and cutting off their root. The story of that is that Germany was defeated in the First World War, which led in its wake to the stagnation of its economy; waves of disorder and anarchy prevailed in it, and disturbances and turmoil descended on its arena, which made the people pass through the worst of its conditions and the hardest of its moments. Hence it was looking forward to someone who would rescue it from it and bring it and the state to the shore of safety and the coast of peace, and steer it on the road of progress and the passage of new development. They found him in Hitler, who assumed the office of prime minister [i.e. chancellor] in 1934 [sic: January 1933]; and as soon as he took power he suspended the constitution and began transforming Germany into a fascist state by implementing all of Mussolini's theories in it, adding to them his nationalist and racial partisanship. When all this blended, the Nazi government resulted in Germany, where the Nazis became the most powerful party in the country. He set about implementing his racist plans built on the basis of extremism against the other races that he regarded as "inferior" in contrast to his superior race. In this regard it is said and believed that he launched fierce, savage attacks against the Jews, and scattered them and expelled them from Germany. There is no doubt that some of these stories have a share of truth, just as there is no doubt that the exaggerations and excesses of the Jews have played a large role in presenting stories of it coloured with various kinds of harm [p. 78] and persecution, massacres and furnaces, with an intensity far exceeding external reality. It is known that the operations of their extermination in Germany and their expulsion from it are called the Holocaust, and many studies and books have been written and published about it, to the point that the public libraries in the West devote a large part to writings on the Holocaust. They have described that holocaust in a style that earns them the sympathy of readers and draws their affection and emotions towards them.

#### Proletarian government

[p. 80] It is in reality another name for the government of the communist state, and is built on the theories and ideas of Karl Marx, foremost among them the theory of "dialectical materialism", which it would be no departure from neutrality to regard as the most important of his theories and ideas.

In summary: that the world, from the beginning of humanity, has continued to be divided into two classes: the class of owners — the wealthy, the rulers and the chiefs — whom he called the "Bourgeois", and the class of labouring workers in the sector of agriculture or industry, whom he called the proletariat. The perpetual struggle between these two classes is the prominent factor and strong element in the wars that broke out among the sons of humanity and the battles whose heat flared among the sons of Adam against one another. The class of owners has continued striving to exert pressure on the class of workers, to suppress their movement, usurp their rights and exploit their labour for its interests — especially after the Industrial Revolution and the invention of machinery, since the production of manufactures continued to be achieved only by the sweat of the worker's brow and the toil of his forehead, while only a very small percentage of its profits accrues to him. To reinforce this idea he invented another theory called the theory of "surplus value", whose meaning is that all the manufactures produced in factories result from two things: raw materials and labour (for cotton became thread after the worker's labour on it, then turned into woven cloth after that). Since raw materials are a divine gift in whose creation man has no hand, labour remains the only strong factor that transforms raw materials into a good form that leads to an increase in its value and a rise in its standing. There is a clear difference and a vast, tangible gap between the value of cotton and the value of what it is transformed into and becomes: ready-made cloth. This only became possible by virtue of the worker's effort and toil; without it the value of cotton would not have increased nor its price risen. Accordingly, and after it was established that the surplus value in the product arose from the worker's labour, it is natural that the worker should be its original deserving owner and first beneficiary. But capitalism usurps this right of his by handing it over to the wealthy financiers and owners of capital, and thus their wealth swells [p. 81] on the ruins of the wreckage of the workers.

He added: this system will not continue much longer; the time has come for the labouring workers to rebel against this system and rise up in revolt against the usurpation of their rights and the injustice to them that they have faced for a long time. Then they will acquire control and establish a government of their own in the world, and their government will be called "the proletarian government". He called on the workers of the whole world to unite against this system; this is called the Communist Manifesto. Although he did not have the fortune of witnessing the government established on the basis of his theories in his lifetime, Lenin was able to establish the first socialist state in Russia after he toppled the system of its Tsar from power, and called it the proletarian government.

Its philosophy focused on the state's ownership of land, factories and other means of production, and that the proletarian government, made up of the class of workers, is the one that will steer the system of government and undertake to exploit the resources of the nation and use the means of production through the system of the planned economy, i.e. it does not delegate to individuals the authority to choose the work they want, produce the manufactures they wish, and use their lands to plant the grains and fruits they wish; rather, the proletarian government is the one that dominates all economic activities, and its planners are the ones who take the decisions relating to the production of goods, their pricing and distribution, and the determination of wages and allowances.

Likewise, since the government was regarded as exclusive to the proletarian class only, it never allowed any other political group nor opposition to its policies nor criticism of its decisions; for if it allowed that, then whoever undertook it would mean nothing other than supporting the bourgeois class, championing it and raising one's voice for its interests. Likewise, since the proletarian government was working to realize the interests of the whole people, if it charged someone with some work, he had to undertake it without any hesitation, argument or procrastination, and for the wage they specified for him, even if it was very meagre in relation to the effort he expended and the toil he endured.

As is known, such a system based on taking away personal [p. 82] freedom will not succeed in achieving its aims unless its proponent employs acts of violence and arts of injustice and aggression. So the totalitarianism that characterized fascism actually extended to the proletariat as well, but under the curtain of protecting the rights of the labouring workers and under the justification of improving their conditions. To avoid the interference and influence of the agents of the wealthy class, they imposed strict restrictions on the media and the press, such that it was no longer possible for anyone to utter a word against the government. Hence it is no unfairness to say that the government Lenin established and administered was among the ugliest forms of dictatorship and the most dangerous of its scenes — which was increased in atrocity by his successor Stalin, who took up its reins following his death.

Its philosophy never managed to improve the condition of the class of workers; for they continued to be crushed and ground by the mill of the proletarian state — which took the place of the owning class, possessing all the resources and means of the state under a system of government so narrow-chested that it did not allow anyone to open his mouth against its policies and plans. In this the poet Muhammad Iqbal said:

> *Zamām-e kār agar mazdūr ke hāthon men ho phir kyā?*
> *Ṭarīq-e kohkan men bhī wohī ḥīle hain parwēzī*

[That is:] Handing the reins of all affairs to the workers results in nothing but transferring the bourgeois [royal] tricks into the hands of the mine-workers.¹

This system remained prevalent in Russia for seventy-four (74) years; but its economic, social, moral and political evils increased to an extent that brought the state to collapse and ruin, so they cried out with one voice rejecting it and getting rid of it.
