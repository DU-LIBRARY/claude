On the other hand, the Pharaoh of Egypt, who was [p. 60] ruling it in the days of the migration of the Children of Israel to it, treated them with tact and kindness. But the Pharaohs who came after him enslaved them and subjected them to their authority, and they were compelled to spend a period of time living in the shadow of servitude to the Pharaohs, until God sent among them our master Moses, upon him be peace — who, alongside calling the Egyptians to divine unity, also declared openly that his mission also aimed at rescuing the Children of Israel and delivering them from the clutches of Pharaoh, and saving them from his torment and harm. Moses, upon him be peace, managed to rescue them from Pharaoh's claws and move them out of Egypt. But they refused to fight against the Amalekites, which brought upon them wandering in the wilderness of Sinai for a long period, until God's mercy took Moses, upon him be peace, and he died there. His successor Joshua, upon him be peace, together with Caleb, upon him be peace (who was a deputy of Joshua), undertook jihad against the Amalekites, and they managed to free a large area of Palestine, which the Children of Israel settled anew and colonized. But a large part of it remained under the control of the Amalekites. When Joshua and Caleb, upon them both be peace, died, they [the Israelites] became an anarchy with no leader to lead them or ruler to govern them, and they remained like the Bedouins among the Arabs in the days of the Jāhiliyya, their life becoming tribal, far from civil and urban [life], without a state or government gathering their scattered parts. However, they would make the man able to settle their disputes satisfactorily, according to their tribal laws, a leader and chief over them, and they called such a man "judge", and their period is called "the Period of the Judges".

Finally, when Samuel, upon him be peace, was sent among them as a prophet, they asked him — having become fed up with this Bedouin life — to call upon God to raise for them a king, under whose leadership they would fight against the Amalekites to free and liberate what remained of the land of Palestine. This is what God mentioned in His words, mighty and exalted:

**"When they said to a prophet of theirs: 'Raise up for us a king, and we will fight in the way of God'"** [al-Baqara, 2:246].

So He raised up for them Ṭālūt (Saul) as king. He was the first king of the Children of Israel who did not obtain the rank of prophethood, whereas the kings before him had combined the two ranks of prophethood and kingship side by side. Ṭālūt undertook to resist the Amalekites. Our master David, upon him be peace, was at that time a young man of tender years who had not yet been sent as a prophet, and it so happened that he joined Ṭālūt's army. A person from the Amalekite army [p. 61] named "Goliath" (Jālūt) demanded a man from the Children of Israel for single combat, and none dared to confront him except David, upon him be peace. So David killed Goliath, which earned him comprehensive love and acceptance that took hold of the hearts of the Children of Israel, to the point that he became their king after Ṭālūt, and God also honoured him with prophethood. Under him the control of the Children of Israel over Palestine was almost completed. He was succeeded by his son Solomon, upon him be peace, under whom the kingdom reached the height of its flourishing, and it was he who built the Temple (Bayt al-Maqdis) by God's permission. The kingdom was called "Judah" after the name of his ancestor. After his death he was succeeded by his son "Rehoboam", who assumed the throne of the kingdom; but — because of his lack of fitness — he did not stop at eliminating the religious spirit in the kingdom, but also inflicted grave harm on its political stability. In this period "Jeroboam" rebelled against his rule and founded an independent kingdom by the name of "Israel". Thus the Children of Israel were divided between two kingdoms: the Jewish kingdom in the south, whose capital was "Jerusalem", and the kingdom of Israel in the north, whose capital was Samaria (what is today called Nablus). A long series of religious and political clashes and conflicts arose between the two kingdoms, in which pagan tendencies took control from time to time and people's turning towards them increased; so prophets were sent to them. When they went to excess in blackening the records of their deeds with evil deeds and were extravagant in transgressing God's limits, God set over them "Nebuchadnezzar", king of "Babylon", who attacked Jerusalem with massive campaigns and destroyed everything in his last campaign. He burned all the copies of the Torah, killed most of their men, and exiled their women along with their last king and those men who remained as slaves and servants to "Babylon", where they lived in that exile once again for a long period a life of humiliation and servitude. In that period our master ʿUzayr (Ezra), upon him be peace, was born. As for what appears in the Qur'an in His words: **"Or [consider] the like of the one who passed by a town that had fallen into ruin upon its roofs"** [al-Baqara, 2:259], many of the commentators held that what is meant by it is Jerusalem following the destruction that took place at the hands of Nebuchadnezzar. This is called "the Period of the Babylonian Captivity".

Then it happened that they repented of their sins, and their prophets prayed for an improvement of their conditions; so God decreed for them that Cyrus (an Iranian king) attacked "Babylon" and conquered it, and following that he permitted the Jews to return to Jerusalem and rebuild it and rebuild the Temple. So they established the kingdom [p. 62] of Judah alone (the kingdom of Israel had been destroyed at the hands of the Assyrians). No sooner had they enjoyed ease and comfort of life in it than they returned once more to their evil deeds and sins to which they had become accustomed, from frivolity and debauchery and even paganism! So God set over them another king named "Antiochus Epiphanes", who put the sword among them and burned all the copies of the Torah; and this is what the Qur'an referred to in His words: **"Then when the final promise came, [We sent your enemies] to sadden your faces and to enter the Temple as they entered it the first time, and to destroy utterly all that they had taken over"** [al-Isrāʾ, 17:7].

As for Nebuchadnezzar's attack, He referred to it in His words: **"So when the first of the two promises came, We sent against you servants of Ours possessed of great might, and they ranged through the homes"** [al-Isrāʾ, 17:5].

At that time Judas Maccabeus rose up among the Children of Israel, formed a group, and was able to recover a large area of Palestine.

If we except this petty state of the Maccabees, the Jews were all at that time scattered and wandering, living an anarchic Bedouin life without any leader or commander. A part of Palestine was ruled by the Romans, and its government was subject to Rome; Jerusalem was one of the provinces of the Roman government, which the Romans called "Judea", and over it was a governor for the Romans.

Such was the state of affairs when our master Jesus, upon him be peace, was born in the days of the first Roman emperor, Augustus, and Herod was king of Judea. Jesus, upon him be peace, presented his call to the Children of Israel, but only a few believed in it; as for the others, they showed him hostility and began to afflict him with persecution and harm. Among those who believed in him were twelve disciples of his, who followed him, supported him and strengthened him to the point of self-sacrifice for that, and a small band of others entered his religion following them. In that period God raised our master Jesus, upon him be peace, to heaven — the one about whom the Christians believe that he was crucified, then brought back to life three days later, then raised to heaven.
